The slope from [Your first analysis](/docs/tutorials/first-analysis) has a factor of safety of 0.984. This tutorial reinforces it with three rows of soil nails.

## 1. Add the nails

Start a new project with **File › New**. Open **Model › Loads & support › Reinforcement**, choose **Soil nail** in the list below the reinforcement table and click **+ Add**, three times.

Each nail runs from **end 1**, the head at the face, to **end 2**, the embedded end. Enter these coordinates (10 m long nails inclined 15° below the horizontal):

| # | x1 | y1 | x2 | y2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 24 | −2 | 14.341 | −4.588 |
| 2 | 29 | −4.5 | 19.341 | −7.088 |
| 3 | 34 | −7 | 24.341 | −9.588 |

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/soil-nails/01-reinforcement.png" alt="The Reinforcement window with three soil nails" caption="Three soil nails with the soil nail defaults: T = 100 kN, spacing S = 1.5 m." />

A soil nail starts with a tensile capacity **T** of 100 kN, an out-of-plane spacing **S** of 1.5 m, a bond strength of 15 kN/m along its whole length and a 50 kN plate at the head. It is passive: its force is mobilised together with the soil and divided by the factor of safety. Click a row to see and change these properties.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/soil-nails/02-model.png" alt="The slope with three nails drawn from the face into the slope" caption="The nails on the edit canvas." />

## 2. Run

Click **▶ Run**.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/soil-nails/03-result.png" alt="The result with the critical circle and the force in each nail" caption="The factor of safety rises from 0.984 to 1.188. Each nail shows the force it carries on the critical surface, per metre run." />

Where the critical surface crosses a nail, the force is the smallest of the tensile capacity, the pullout resistance on the head side (bond plus the plate) and the pullout resistance on the embedded side (bond), divided by the spacing. The upper nail, with the longest anchorage behind the surface, carries the most.

## 3. Try it

- Reduce the spacing to 1 m: more nails per metre, a higher factor of safety.
- Change the nails to **Active**: their force is no longer divided by the factor of safety, as for prestressed anchors.
- Lengthen the lowest nail, and see whether its force grows.

More in [Reinforcement and piles](/docs/reinforcement).
