This tutorial uses the example model that every new project starts with: a homogeneous slope, 10 m high, from a published benchmark (ACADS problem 1(a), Giam & Donald 1989). You look at its geometry and material, run the analysis, read the result and export it.

## 1. Start a new project

Start cSlope, or choose **File › New**. The project contains one model, *T-ACADS Simple*, shown on the canvas in edit mode.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/01-window.png" alt="The cSlope window in edit mode with the Model tab open and the example slope on the canvas" caption="The cSlope window: the Model tab of the ribbon, the Explorer with the model, the canvas and the status bar." />

The slope has its crest at y = 0 and its toe at y = −10, with a 1V:2H face between x = 20 and x = 40. The soil is one Mohr-Coulomb material.

## 2. Look at the geometry

Open **Model › Boundaries › Exterior**. The window lists the points of the exterior boundary. Click a row and the point is highlighted on the canvas. Drag a point on the canvas and watch its coordinates change in the table, then undo with <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Z</Kbd>.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/02-exterior.png" alt="The Exterior Boundary window with six points" caption="The exterior boundary: six points around the section." />

The top of the boundary is the ground surface; slip surfaces start and end on it.

## 3. Check the material

Open **Model › Materials › Define**. The model has one material, *M1*: Mohr-Coulomb with γ = 20 kN/m³, φ = 19.6° and c = 3 kPa.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/03-materials.png" alt="The Define Materials window with material M1" caption="Define Materials: the material list at the top, the selected material's properties below." />

Any change you make applies at once. Leave the values as they are to compare your result with the published one below.

## 4. Set where surfaces may start and end

Open **Model › Analysis › Search limits**. *Restrict entry/exit ranges* is ticked: slip surfaces enter the ground between x = 17 and 22, at the crest, and leave it between x = 37 and 43 at the toe.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/04-search-limits.png" alt="The Search limits window with entry and exit ranges" caption="The entry and exit ranges." />

With the window open, the ranges show as markers on the canvas, and you can drag them.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/05-search-limits-canvas.png" alt="The slope with the entry range on the crest and the exit range at the toe marked" caption="The entry range (green) on the crest and the exit range (red) at the toe." />

## 5. Choose the method

Open **Model › Analysis › Options**. The method is **Morgenstern-Price** with a **half-sine** interslice function and 30 slices.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/06-options.png" alt="The Options window" caption="The analysis options." />

## 6. Run the analysis

Click **▶ Run** on the floating toolbar at the bottom of the canvas. After a moment the canvas switches to result mode.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/07-result.png" alt="Result mode with the critical slip circle, the result summary on the right and the Results tab open" caption="The result: the critical circle with its slices, and the summary on the right." />

## 7. Read the result

The summary on the right shows a **Min FOS** of 0.984, found among some 1600 analysed surfaces. The referee answer published for this problem is 1.00; the factor of safety from Morgenstern–Price and similar methods is 0.98 to 0.99.

Choose **Results › Display**, then **All surfaces** in the surfaces list, to see every analysed surface coloured by its factor of safety:

<Figure src="/media/generated/result-all-surfaces.png" alt="All analysed slip surfaces coloured from red to blue by factor of safety" caption="All surfaces, from red (lowest factor of safety) to blue." />

Switch back to **Critical only**.

## 8. Compare methods

Click **Edit**, open **Model › Analysis › Options**, choose **Bishop** and run again. For a circle in a homogeneous slope, Bishop's value is very close to Morgenstern–Price's (0.985). **Janbu** gives a lower value; tick *Apply correction factor f0* to apply Janbu's correction.

## 9. Export and save

Choose **Results › Export › PDF** to save the result sheet as a PDF. The title on the sheet comes from the model's properties: right-click the model in the Explorer and choose **Properties** to change it.

Save the project with **File › Save**. The file contains the model and its result.
