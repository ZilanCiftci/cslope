The result view is a print sheet. This tutorial lays it out for a report and exports it.

Start a new project with **File › New** and click **▶ Run**.

## 1. The paper

Click an empty part of the sheet to select the paper. The **Sheet** section of the sidebar shows its settings:

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/reporting/01-paper.png" alt="The result view with the paper selected and its properties in the sidebar" caption="The paper selected: extents, ticks, paper size and orientation in the sidebar." />

- **Left x**, **Right x**, **Bottom y**, **Top y** (m): the part of the model on the sheet. **Use model bounds** fits the whole model.
- **Paper size** and **Paper orientation**.

**Results › Display › Sheet settings** does the same: it selects the paper and shows these properties in the sidebar.

## 2. Annotations

The sheet of a new project's model already has text for the title ("New slope analysis") and the subtitle (empty), a line with the calculated factor of safety, and a material table. The title and subtitle come from **Model properties**: right-click the model in the Models panel to change them. Add more from **Results › Annotations**: text, a material table, a plot of slice data, or a color bar.

Show all surfaces (**Results › Display**, surfaces list, **All surfaces**) and add a **color bar** to explain the colors:

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/reporting/02-color-bar.png" alt="All surfaces colored by factor of safety, with a color bar" caption="All surfaces with a color bar." />

Drag annotations to place them. Select one to edit its text, font, size and color in the sidebar. A text can show values from the model: `#Title`, `#Client`, `#Revision`, `#Method`, `#FOS` (F of the critical surface), `#FOS3D` and others, and any parameter as `#name`; **Insert variable** under the text lists them. See [Reporting](/docs/reporting#text-fields) for the list.

The **Layers** list in the sidebar shows the annotations and the display options turned on, topmost first. Drag a row by its grip to draw it above or below the others, for example a text over the material table ([Layers](/docs/reporting#layers)).

## 3. Reuse the layout

**Results › Templates › Export template…** saves the layout (annotations, display options, paper and extents) as a file. **Import template…** applies it to another model, so every model in a report gets the same title block.

## 4. Export

**Results › Export › Export** saves the sheet as a vector PDF. The ▾ next to it has the other formats:

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/reporting/04-export.png" alt="The export menu with PDF, PNG, Excel and CSV options" caption="Export: the sheet as PDF or PNG, the slices of the critical surface as Excel or CSV." />

The slice table lists every slice of the critical surface with its geometry, weight, strength, pore pressure and forces, for a calculation appendix.
