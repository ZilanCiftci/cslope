Start a new project with **File › New** and click **▶ Run**. The factor of safety is 0.984.

## 1. Sensitivity

Choose **Results › Plots › Sensitivity**. The first input, the unit weight of M1, is chosen, from 16 to 24 kN/m³ in 9 values. Click **Run**: cSlope runs the full analysis for each value.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/probabilistic/01-sensitivity.png" alt="The sensitivity panel with a chart of factor of safety against unit weight" caption="Factor of safety against unit weight, with the line at 1.0." />

The factor of safety reaches 1.0 at a unit weight of about 18.5 kN/m³. Choose another input under **Vary**, such as c or φ, to see which one matters most.

## 2. Probability of failure

Close the panel and choose **Results › Plots › Probabilistic**. The panel starts with one variable, the cohesion of M1 (c = 3 kPa, σ = 0.75 kPa). Click **+ Add variable** once to add the unit weight (γ = 20 kN/m³, σ = 1 kN/m³). Both are normally distributed. Choose another property or σ in each row if you like.

Leave **Taylor series** selected and click **Run**.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/probabilistic/02-probabilistic.png" alt="The probabilistic panel with the results of a Taylor series analysis" caption="Taylor series: the standard deviation of the factor of safety, the reliability index and the probability of failure." />

With a mean factor of safety below 1.0, the reliability index is negative and the probability of failure is above 50 %. The table at the bottom shows how much each variable changes the factor of safety: here the cohesion matters more than the unit weight.

**Monte Carlo** gives the same quantities from many random samples, and a histogram of the factor of safety.
