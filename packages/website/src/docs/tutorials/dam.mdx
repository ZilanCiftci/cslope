This tutorial uses the Talbingo dam, a rockfill dam with a clay core and a published benchmark for slope stability programs. Open **Help › Benchmarks** and click **Talbingo Dam**.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/dam/01-model.png" alt="A dam cross-section with rockfill shells, transition and filter zones and a clay core" caption="Rockfill shells (yellow), transition and filter zones, and the clay core." />

## 1. The zones

Open **Model › Materials › Define**. The rockfill, transition and filter are frictional (φ = 45°, γ = 20.4 kN/m³); the core is a clay with c = 85 kPa and φ = 23° (γ = 18.1 kN/m³).

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/dam/02-materials.png" alt="The Define Materials window with four dam materials" caption="Four materials, one per zone." />

## 2. Direction

This section fails towards the left. In **Model › Analysis › Options**, **Analysis Direction** is set to **Right to left**. For a slope where either face could fail, tick **Analyse both sliding directions** instead.

## 3. Run

Click **▶ Run**.

<Figure src="/media/generated/result-dam.png" alt="The critical slip surface through the upstream shell of the dam" caption="The critical surface lies in the rockfill of the face." />

The factor of safety is 1.95, equal to the published value.

## 4. Check the equilibrium

Morgenstern–Price finds the factor of safety where moment and force equilibrium give the same value. Open **Results › Plots › Results Plot** and choose **Lambda vs Fm/Ff**:

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/dam/04-lambda.png" alt="Factor of safety from moment and force equilibrium against lambda" caption="Fm (moment) and Ff (force) against λ. They meet at the factor of safety." />

In a frictional shell the two curves lie close together: the result hardly depends on the interslice forces. Where they diverge strongly, the choice of interslice function matters more.
