This tutorial uses a published example with a water table, from Arai & Tagyo (1985). Open it from **Help › Examples** and click **Arai & Tagyo (1985)** in the Models panel.

## 1. The model

A 20 m high slope in one soil (γ = 18.82 kN/m³, c = 41.65 kPa, φ = 15°), with a water table that falls from 3 m below the crest to the ground surface at the toe.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/water-table/01-model.png" alt="A slope with a blue water table falling from below the crest to the toe" caption="The water table (blue, with ▽ symbols) and the search ranges on the crest and at the toe." />

## 2. The piezometric line

Open **Model › Water & parameters › Piezometric lines**. The model has one line, *Water Table*, with five points.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/water-table/02-piezo.png" alt="The Piezometric lines window with a line of five points" caption="The piezometric line and its points. Free water above ground is on." />

To make your own: click **+ Add line** for a new line across the model, then edit its points in the table, or click the line on the canvas and drag its points. Right-click the line on the canvas to add a point.

Where the line is above the ground surface, **Free water above ground (ponding)** lets the water stand on the slope and load it. Here the line meets the ground at the toe, so no water is ponded.

## 3. Run

Click **▶ Run**.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/water-table/03-result.png" alt="Result mode with the critical circle crossing the water table" caption="The critical circle. The water table lowers the effective normal stress on the slip surface." />

cSlope finds a factor of safety of 1.113 with Morgenstern-Price. The published values are 1.138 (Arai & Tagyo) and 1.118 (from a commercial program's verification manual).

## 4. Pore pressure along the slip surface

Choose **Results › Plots › Results plot** and set **Y axis** to **Pore pressure**:

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/water-table/04-pore-pressure.png" alt="A plot of pore pressure against slice number, rising to about 106 kPa in the middle" caption="Pore pressure at the base of each slice, highest where the circle is deepest below the water table." />

The plot window can show any quantity along any analyzed surface, not only the critical one.

## 5. Try it

- Click the water table on the canvas, drag its points down by a few meters and run again: the factor of safety rises.
- Choose **Phreatic correction** under *Pore pressure below an inclined water line* in the options, run again and compare.
