## Method

| Setting | Options | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Method** | Bishop, Janbu, Morgenstern-Price | Morgenstern-Price |
| **Slices** | 10–500 | 30 |
| **Tolerance** | 0.001–0.1 | 0.001 |

- **Bishop**: Bishop simplified (moment equilibrium).
- **Janbu**: Janbu simplified (force equilibrium). Tick **Apply correction factor f0** to apply Janbu's correction.
- **Morgenstern-Price**: full equilibrium. Choose the **Interslice function**: `constant` (Spencer's method), `half-sine` (default), `clipped-sine`, `trapezoidal` or `data-point-specified`. For the last one, enter the points as `x,f; x,f` with x from 0 (left end of the surface) to 1 (right end), for example `0,0; 0.2,0.6; 0.5,1; 0.8,0.6; 1,0`.

The [theory](/docs/theory) page gives the equations. The **Tolerance** is the convergence limit on the factor of safety.

<Callout type="warning" title="Bishop on non-circular surfaces">
  Bishop's simplified method is defined for circular surfaces (Bishop 1955). On a non-circular surface (a polyline you draw, or one from non-circular optimization) cSlope takes the moments about an axis point, and because the method does not satisfy horizontal force equilibrium the factor of safety depends on where that point is. On a composite surface along a weak seam it ranges from 1.36 to 1.41 for different axis points, while Spencer's method gives 1.379 for all of them. cSlope still computes it, and the results then show a warning. Use Spencer or Morgenstern-Price for non-circular surfaces.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
  Thirty slices are enough for most slopes; the steep ends of a circle are cut into narrower slices automatically. With thin layers or a tension crack, check that more slices do not change the result.
</Callout>

## Tension crack

- **Tension crack depth (m)**: a vertical crack at the upper end of the slip surface, in which the soil carries no shear. 0 means no crack.
- **Tension crack depth from the soil (Rankine)**: compute the depth from the strength at the crest, $z_c = 2c/(\gamma\sqrt{K_a}) - q/\gamma$, instead of entering it. The depth field then shows *Auto*.
- **Crack at base angle (°)**: a crack where the slip surface flattens to this inclination. From the entry, the part of the surface steeper than the angle is cut off and the surface ends at a vertical crack there, as deep as the surface is below the ground at that point. Often 45° + φ/2 (the active wedge) or about 60°. 0 means none. With a crack depth as well, the crack further from the entry governs; the water in the crack fills its share of the depth there. When a surface enters steeper than 70° and the slices on that part hold only by tension at the base (any method), or Spencer or Morgenstern-Price finds no complete equilibrium there, the result (or the message that no surface was found) says so and points here: cohesion along a nearly vertical base can only hold the slice with tension at its base, and cutting that part off usually gives a solution.
- **Water in crack (%)**: how full the crack is with water, from 0 to 100 %. The water pushes on the sliding mass.

## Partial factors

Tick **Partial factors (design values)** for a design check with partial factors. The strength parameters are divided by their factors and the loads multiplied by theirs before the analysis:

| Factor | Applies to | Swedish practice (EKS/IEG) | EN 1997-1 DA3, M2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **γcu** | Undrained shear strength | 1.5 | 1.4 |
| **γc'** | Effective cohesion | 1.3 | 1.25 |
| **γφ'** | tan φ' | 1.3 | 1.25 |
| **γQ** | Distributed and line loads | 1.3 | 1.3 |

Ticking the box fills in the Swedish set at once. Choose another set from **Preset…** or enter the factors yourself.

**Swedish practice from safety class and η** forms the factors from the safety class and the conversion factors η, as design values $X_d = \eta X_k / \gamma_M$ and variable loads $Q_d = \gamma_Q \gamma_d Q_k$:

| Input | Factors |
| --- | --- |
| **Safety class** SK1, SK2, SK3 | $\gamma_d$ = 0.83, 0.91, 1.0 on the variable loads |
| **η (Su)** | $\gamma_{cu} = 1.5/\eta$ |
| **η (c′, φ′)** | $\gamma_{c'} = \gamma_{\varphi'} = 1.3/\eta$ |
| **γQ / γd** (1.3, 1.4 or 1.5) | $\gamma_Q$ = this × $\gamma_d$ |

Safety class 3 with η = 1 and γQ/γd = 1.3 gives the Swedish set above. Which η and which load factor apply is decided by the governing document (for example 1.4 · γd is used for traffic loads in some Swedish guidance). The factors follow the inputs; entering a factor by hand leaves the inputs and keeps the factors as they are. The summary lists the inputs with the factors.

Which factor applies depends on the material. Undrained models, and Mohr-Coulomb materials with φ = 0, are treated as undrained and take γcu. For lime-cement columns each part takes its own factor: the undrained strength of soil and columns takes γcu, c′ of the columns γc', and tan φ′ of the columns γφ′. A combined material (Mohr-Coulomb with Su, or combined S = f(depth)) takes γcu on Su and γc' and γφ' on its drained strength. Other materials take γc' and γφ'. The [strength from suction](/docs/materials#strength-from-suction) (φb) takes γc', like c′. With partial factors the result is an **over-design factor** on the design values: 1.0 or more means the design is satisfied. The summary shows it as *Min FOS (design values)*.

## Seismic load (pseudostatic)

**kh** and **kv** under *Seismic (pseudostatic)* add an earthquake load: each slice carries $k_h W$ horizontally in the sliding direction at its center of gravity, and $k_v W$ vertically (positive downward, adding to the weight; check both signs). The factor of safety with them is the pseudostatic factor of safety.

## 3D effects (temporary slopes)

Tick **3D effects (SGI Vägledning 8)** under **Design** to add the factor of safety of a temporary excavation opened in stages, after SGI's guidance (Vägledning 8, *Temporära slänter*, 2024, section 2.1). The stage of length $L$ is bounded by two plane end faces, whose undrained shear strength adds resisting moment to each circular surface of the 2D analysis:

$$F_P = F_{2D} + \frac{2 \int c_u\, r\, dA}{L\, M_E}, \qquad F_{3D} = F_{2D} + k \cdot 0.75\,(F_P - F_{2D})$$

with $M_E$ the surface's driving moment, the integral over the end face's cohesive soil ($r$ the distance to the circle's center) and $k = 1$ for infinitely overstrong adjacent stability (eq. 2-3) or $k = 1 - F_{2D,krit,min} / F_{2D,överstark,min}$ for finite (eq. 2-4).

- **Stage length L (m)**: the length of the excavation open at a time.
- **Adjacent F2D**: $F_{2D,överstark,min}$ of the stretches beside the stage; leave it blank for infinitely overstrong adjacent stability.
- **Not in the end faces**: materials left out of the end faces, for example a fissured dry crust. Friction soil never counts.

The result is the smallest $F_{3D}$ over the circular surfaces of the search, which is generally on a shallower surface than the critical 2D one. The end faces take the undrained strength without anisotropy (its direct value), for a combined clay its Su, as design values when partial factors are on. The guidance applies to total stress analysis of normally to lightly overconsolidated clay; a 3D factor more than 25 % above the critical 2D factor is to be judged with care, and the result shows a warning then.

## Finite elements (experimental)

Tick **Run strength reduction (FE)** under **Finite elements** in the options to run a finite element analysis after the limit equilibrium analysis; its settings appear once it is ticked. It runs the limit analysis bounds, Davis B and strength reduction, with the plastic zone on the canvas. It is **experimental**: the analysis is still under development, it has fewer verification problems than limit equilibrium, its results are not verified for design and cannot be guaranteed, and they may change between versions. Base the design on the limit equilibrium result; the options, the result panel and the canvas mark the finite element results as experimental.

### Strength reduction

**Run strength reduction (FE)** (off by default) reduces c and tan φ by a factor F until the slope can no longer be brought into equilibrium. The soil is elastic–perfectly plastic and the mesh is made of six-node triangles. No slip surface is assumed: the failure mechanism comes out of the analysis, and the plastic zone on the canvas shows it. An analysis takes from seconds to minutes.

### Flow rule

- **Davis B (recommended)**, the default: associated flow with the strengths reduced for the dilation angle (Tschuchnigg et al. 2015). It is fast and stable at high friction angles, and on the safe side: with ψ = 0, φ ≤ 30° and F below 1 it can lie 10–35 % under non-associated flow.
- **Associated (ψ = φ)**: the dilation angle equals the friction angle.
- **Non-associated (ψ)**: flow with ψ < φ. It is closer to other finite element programs at moderate friction angles but several times slower, and can give an oscillating, non-unique factor of safety at high friction angles (Tschuchnigg et al. 2015).

**Dilation angle ψ (°)** applies to every soil and is at most φ: usually 0 for clays and loose soils; for dense sands Bolton (1986) gives φ_peak − φ_cv ≈ 0.8ψ.

### Solver and convergence

Davis B and associated flow are always solved by Newton's method. For non-associated flow, **Solver** chooses:

- **Viscoplastic** (default): the viscoplastic iteration of Griffiths & Lane.
- **Newton**: Newton's method with the unsymmetric tangent, the reduction factor raised in increments (the implicit method of most finite element programs). In the verification cases it is within about 1 % of the viscoplastic factor, and usually slower.

**Convergence** is the convergence test of the viscoplastic iteration:

- **Increment** (default): the displacement change of an iteration is below 10⁻⁴ of the displacement (Griffiths & Lane).
- **Equilibrium**: in addition, the stresses returned to the yield surface must balance the load to 0.1 % (the unbalanced-force test of other programs). The iteration is then Anderson-accelerated, so that trials near failure converge instead of running out of iterations. It comes closer to other programs at high friction angles (on Pruska's slopes −3.4 % against −4.7 %) and takes about 1.5 times the run time.

Newton's method always tests equilibrium (a residual of 10⁻⁶).

### Mesh and stiffness

- **Element size (m)**: the size of the first mesh. Empty (the default): a twentieth of the model height, finer in thin layers.
- **Young's modulus E (kPa)** (default 100,000) and **Poisson's ratio ν** (default 0.3) of every soil. E scales the displacements and barely affects the factor of safety.
- **Mesh refinements** (0–3, default 1): adaptive refinements where the plastic dissipation at failure is largest. They stop early when F changes by less than 0.01, and each roughly doubles the run time. They are not used with non-associated flow: its factor falls on every refinement without a limit, so it is computed on the first mesh. The limit analysis bounds refine their own mesh.
- **Tension cut-off** (on by default): no tensile effective stress (a Rankine cut-off). Without it, cohesive soil carries tension near the crest and the factor of safety can come out several percent higher.

### Pore pressures

**Pore pressures (FE)**: **From the water table** (default), as in the limit equilibrium analysis, or **Steady seepage** (experimental). With steady seepage the water table gives the reservoir and the tail water where it lies at or above the ground, and the phreatic surface and the seepage face come from the flow. All soils have one permeability, the unsaturated flow follows van Genuchten–Mualem with the parameters of a loam, and rigid materials are nearly impervious.

### Initial stresses

- **Gravity loading** (default): the weight is applied in one step from zero stress.
- **K0 procedure**: the horizontal stresses are K0 times the vertical effective stress from the overburden, as for level ground; under a slope, the out-of-balance force of these stresses is applied with the first solution. **K0** applies to every soil; empty, it is 1 − sin φ′ (Jaky), and ν/(1 − ν) for undrained and rigid materials. Overconsolidated clays can have K0 above 1. For a cut slope, **Original ground (m)** is the ground level before the excavation: the K0 stresses are those under this level, and their out-of-balance force unloads the excavation. Empty: the present ground surface.

With associated flow and Davis B the factor of safety does not depend on the initial stresses (the theorems of limit analysis); with non-associated flow, and for the displacements, it does.

**Construction stages** builds the materials in stages: stage 0 first, each later stage on the one before, solved at F = 1 (for example an embankment in layers). The strength reduction starts from the last stage.

### Limit analysis bounds

**Limit analysis bounds (the FE factor of safety)** (on in new projects) computes the lower and upper bounds of limit analysis: rigid–perfectly plastic soil with associated flow, solved as second-order cone programs. A statically admissible stress field (the lower bound) and a kinematically admissible mechanism (the upper bound) bracket the factor of safety, and they converge as the mesh is refined. They are then the finite element factor of safety, and the strength reduction is a comparison with other programs.

The same bounds are also computed with Davis B's strength reduced for the dilation angle, which stands for non-associated flow. The bounds run on a mesh graded as the strength reduction's but twice as coarse, which is then refined where the gap between the bounds lies until the gap is below 3 % (at most three times). They add several minutes to the run, up to about 25.

### Finite element results

The result summary lists, under *Finite elements*:

- **FOS, limit analysis (FE)**: the range between the lower bound (a statically admissible stress field) and the upper bound (a kinematically admissible mechanism) of rigid–perfectly plastic soil with associated flow, with the number of elements, the refinements where the gap between the bounds lay and the time. The factor of safety lies between them; unlike strength reduction with non-associated flow, it converges as the mesh is refined.
- **Davis B (non-associated)**: the same bounds with Davis B's reduced strength for the dilation angle, the associated material that stands for non-associated flow and usually a conservative estimate of it. The associated range above bounds the non-associated soil's factor of safety from above (Radenković 1962); a wide gap between the two means that the flow rule matters for the slope.
- **FOS, strength reduction (FE)**, or **Strength reduction (comparison)** when the bounds were found: the largest factor on c and tan φ at which the slope is still in equilibrium. With non-associated flow it is the largest factor at which the iteration still finds equilibrium, as other programs compute it; it has no limit as the mesh is refined (it falls on every refinement), so it is computed on the first mesh and depends on its size. Compare it with other programs' results, not as the factor of safety.

## Running the analysis

Click **▶ Run** on the floating toolbar. cSlope analyzes the active model in the background and switches to result mode when it is done. The status bar shows *Analyzing* meanwhile, and the button becomes **■ Cancel**.

The menu next to Run has:

- **Run all**: analyze every model in the project, several at a time.
- **Clear results of this model**: clear the result of the active model and return to edit mode.
- **Clear results of all models**: clear the results of every model.

If the model has a problem, for example an expression that cannot be evaluated, the analysis does not start and a message says why. If the analysis fails, a banner over the canvas says why, with a button to the cause where there is one (**Search limits**); the status bar and the result summary say *Analysis failed*. A very large search stops after a time limit that grows with the number of iterations.

<Callout type="note">
  Changing anything that affects the analysis clears the model's result, so a result always belongs to the model as it is.
</Callout>
