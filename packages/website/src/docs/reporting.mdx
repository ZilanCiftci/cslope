The result view is a print sheet: what you see is what the PDF export contains.

## Paper and extents

Click an empty part of the sheet to select the paper and edit it in the **Sheet** section of the **Results** sidebar. **Results › Display › Sheet settings** does the same: it selects the paper, opens the sidebar and brings the paper's settings into view.

- **Paper size** A4 to A0 and **orientation** portrait or landscape.
- **Plot extents**: *Left x*, *Right x*, *Bottom y* and *Top y* (m), the part of the model shown on the sheet. The scale of the drawing follows from the extents and the paper. **Use model bounds** fits the whole model.
- **Grid & ticks**: the tick spacing (*Auto* when empty) and the number of minor ticks. Tick **Grid** to draw grid lines; **Grid spacing** sets their distance (the tick spacing when empty).

Zooming and panning in result mode move the sheet on the screen; they do not change the drawing.

## Annotations

**Results › Annotations** adds:

- **Text**: a text block. Choose font, size, bold, italic and color.
- **Material table**: the materials with their properties. Choose the text size and the columns.
- **Plot**: a chart of slice data along the critical surface, placed on the sheet. Both axes have round ticks with their values, and grid lines at the ticks.
- **Color bar**: the factor-of-safety color scale of the trial surfaces (*All surfaces* and *Filter by FOS*). Its settings in the sidebar set the scale, which also colors the surfaces on the sheet, so the drawing and the bar always agree. **Range**: *Automatic* runs from the critical factor of safety to a cap near it (twice the critical value, or one unit above it), so the colors are spread over the surfaces near the critical one; *Fixed* takes a minimum and a maximum of your own; *Follow the surface filter* takes the span of **Surfaces › Filter by FOS**. Surfaces beyond either end get the end color, and the tick there reads ≥ or ≤. **Color scheme**: red–yellow–green–blue (the default), traffic light, warm to cool, viridis, grayscale, or *Custom* with a color for the low end, one for the high end and an optional middle color. Without a color bar the drawing uses the automatic range and the default scheme.

A sheet has one material table and one color bar: their buttons toggle, so a second click removes the table or the bar. Both are drawn on an opaque plate, so what lies under them does not show through. On screen their text is never smaller than about 9 px: on a small sheet (a small window at 100 %) the table and the bar's values are drawn larger than on paper, so they stay readable; the PDF keeps their size on the paper. A new color bar is placed at the right edge of the drawing, just inside the frame, right below the material table as it is drawn for the model's materials, columns, text size and paper (where there is no room below the table, to its left at the top; without a table, a third of the way down). Drag it where it covers nothing.

Drag annotations to place them; their position is stored as a percentage of the sheet, so they stay in place on any paper size. Select several with <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+click and right-click to align them. The arrow keys nudge the selection, <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> removes it. The [**Layers**](#layers) list in the sidebar shows all of them; the last section of the sidebar edits the selected one and is named after it (*Text*, *Material table*, *Plot*, *Color bar*, *Reference line*; *Selection* for a mix). Selecting an object scrolls that section into view.

### Text fields

Text annotations can show values from the model. They are replaced when the sheet is drawn. **Insert variable** under the *Text* field lists them with their current values and inserts the chosen one at the cursor:

| Field | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| `#Title`, `#Subtitle`, `#Client`, `#ProjectNumber`, `#Revision`, `#Author`, `#Checker`, `#Date`, `#Description` | The model's project information (right-click the model in the Models panel, then *Model properties*) |
| `#Method` | The analysis method |
| `#FOS`, `#MinFOS` | The factor of safety |
| `#FOS3D` | The smallest 3D factor of safety after SGI Vägledning 8 ([3D effects](/docs/analysis#3d-effects-temporary-slopes)); *N/A* when the analysis has no 3D result |
| `#name` | The value of the parameter *name* |
| `{{expression}}` | The value of an expression with parameters, for example `{{H / 2}}` |

`\n` starts a new line. A new model starts with a title (`#Title`), a subtitle (`#Subtitle`) and a material table.

### Reference lines

A reference line is a line in model coordinates drawn over the result: a slip circle read from another program's printout, a slip surface interpreted from a paper for a benchmark. It is drawn under the critical surface in a color no surface uses, in the PDF and PNG exports too.

**Results › Annotations › Reference line** adds a **Polyline** or a **Circle** through the middle of the sheet. A circle is drawn as the arc below the ground, like a slip circle (*Drawn: Whole circle* in the properties shows all of it). The curve icon at the end of a row in **Model › Analysis › Custom surfaces** copies a custom surface as a reference line labeled *Custom surface n*; so does *Add as reference line* in the right-click menu of a custom plane in the edit view.

Click a line to select it, then drag its points, a circle's center or its rim; drag the line itself to move it whole. Right-click a polyline to **Add point** on a segment or **Delete point** at a vertex, or to delete the line; <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> removes the selection. Each drag is one undo step. The **Reference line** section of the sidebar edits the label (drawn near the line, for example `F = 0.664 (other program)`), the points as `x, y; x, y; …` or the center and radius, the color (*Theme* follows the light or dark theme), the dash, the width and whether the line also shows in the edit view.

Reference lines belong to the model: they are saved with it and left out of view templates.

## Layers

The **Layers** list in the result sidebar shows what the sheet draws, topmost first, as in a drawing program: every annotation, the display options turned on under **Results › Display** (*Soil colors*, *Surfaces*, *Custom surfaces*, *Slices*, *Center*, *FOS label*, and with a finite element result *Plastic zone* and *FE mesh*), the *Critical surface* and each drawing shown in results. A display option turned off in the ribbon is not in the list.

- Drag a row by its grip, or focus it and press <Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>↑</Kbd> or <Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>↓</Kbd>, to draw it above or below the others, for example the soil colors over the slices, or a text under the material table. The result view and the PDF export follow the order. The order is saved with the model and each change is one undo step; a display option turned off and on again comes back at its place, and a new annotation goes on top.
- Click an annotation to select it (<Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+click adds it to the selection). The button at the end of the row removes an annotation, turns a display option off as the ribbon does, or takes a drawing out of the results (*Show in results*). The critical surface is always drawn.
- Click a display option (or focus it and press <Kbd>Enter</Kbd>) to set how it is drawn; its properties open in the last section of the sidebar, headed with its name:

  | Layer | Properties |
  | --- | --- |
  | FOS label | Font, size, bold, decimals, color (custom surfaces' labels use the same font) |
  | Soil colors | Opacity |
  | Critical surface, slices | Color, line width |
  | Center | Color, dot radius, radius lines on or off |
  | Surfaces | Line width, opacity |
  | Custom surfaces | Color, line width, label size |
  | FE mesh | Color, line width, opacity (result view only, as the PDF has no finite element layers) |
  | Plastic zone | Color, opacity (result view only) |

  An empty field or **Default** draws the layer as by default: the theme's ink on screen, black in the PDF. A chosen color is drawn as chosen in both themes and in the PDF. **Reset to default** clears the layer's settings. Each change is one undo step; the settings are saved with the model and in templates.

A model without an order of its own draws in the default order: the soil colors and drawings at the bottom, then the finite element layers, the reference lines, the surfaces, the critical surface with its slices, center and FOS label, and the annotations on the sheet on top. The labels of the reference lines and the reinforcement forces stay over the model's layers.

## Templates

A template stores the layout of the sheet: annotations, display options and their layer settings, paper and extents. Reference lines are not part of it.

- **Results › Templates › Export template…** saves the layout of the active model as `view-template.json`.
- **Results › Templates › Import template…** applies a saved layout to the active model.

Use a template to give every model and every project the same title block and layout.

## Export

**Results › Export › Export** saves the sheet as a vector PDF. The menu next to it also has:

| Export | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Export as PDF…** | The sheet as a vector PDF, named after the model's title or project number |
| **Export as PNG…** | The sheet as an image |
| **Slice data (Excel)…** | The slices of the critical surface as an .xlsx table |
| **Slice data (CSV)…** | The same as CSV |
| **Slice data (CSV, decimal comma)…** | CSV with `;` as the separator and `,` as the decimal mark |

The slice table has one row per slice with its geometry (x left, right and mid, width, top and base elevation, height, area, base angle and length), weight, surface load, base material, c, φ, pore pressure, effective normal force and shear strength. The header rows name the model, method, factor of safety and critical surface.
