cSlope is limit equilibrium software for the stability of slopes, embankments and cuttings. This documentation explains each part of the program and walks through complete analyses in the tutorials.

<Figure media="criticalSurface" caption="A result view: the critical slip circle of a homogeneous slope with its slices, the factor of safety and the material table." />

## Where to start

- New to cSlope: [install it](/docs/installation), read [the cSlope window](/docs/interface), then follow the tutorial [Your first analysis](/docs/tutorials/first-analysis).
- Looking for a particular dialog or option: use the menu on the left. The pages follow the order in which a model is built.
- Checking how a result is computed: the [theory](/docs/theory) page gives the equations, and the [verification](/verification) page compares cSlope with published solutions.

## How an analysis is built

1. **[Geometry](/docs/geometry)**: draw the exterior boundary of the section and the boundaries between materials, or import them from a DXF drawing.
2. **[Materials](/docs/materials)**: define the soils and assign one to each region.
3. **[Water](/docs/water)**: add a piezometric line, and lines for individual materials if needed.
4. **[Loads](/docs/loads)** and **[reinforcement](/docs/reinforcement)**: add distributed and line loads, anchors, nails, geosynthetics and piles.
5. **[Search](/docs/search)**: set where slip surfaces may enter and exit, or define your own.
6. **[Analysis options](/docs/analysis)**: choose the method, the number of slices and any tension crack or partial factors, and run the analysis.
7. **[Results](/docs/results)** and **[reporting](/docs/reporting)**: inspect the critical surface and the forces on its slices, annotate the view and export it.

[Sensitivity and probabilistic analysis](/docs/sensitivity) build on a finished model.

## Units

cSlope works in SI units: lengths in metres, stresses and strengths in kPa, unit weights in kN/m³ and forces in kN per metre run. The unit weight of water is 9.81 kN/m³.
