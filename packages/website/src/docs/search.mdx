cSlope looks for the **critical slip surface**: the one with the lowest factor of safety. By default it generates circular surfaces between an entry range near the crest and an exit range near the toe, analyzes each, and then refines the search around the best ones. A [block search](#block-search) tries surfaces through regions you choose instead.

<Figure media="allSurfaces" caption="Every analyzed surface, colored by its factor of safety. The critical surface is the lowest." />

## Search limits

Open **Model › Analysis › Search limits** and tick **Restrict entry/exit ranges** to choose where surfaces may start and end:

- **Entry range (crest side)**: Min X and Max X of the points where surfaces enter the ground.
- **Exit range (toe side)**: Min X and Max X of the points where they leave it.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/first-analysis/05-search-limits-canvas.png" alt="A slope with the entry range on the crest and the exit range at the toe" caption="The entry range (green) and the exit range (red) on the canvas." />

With the window open you can also drag the range markers on the canvas. Without limits, surfaces may enter and exit anywhere on the ground surface.

Restrict the ranges to the part of the slope you are assessing, and keep them wide enough that the critical surface does not end at a limit. If it does, widen the range and run again.

## Search settings

The size of the search is set in **Model › Analysis › Options**:

| Setting | Range | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Iterations** | 500–100 000 | 1000 | Approximate number of trial circles: entry points × exit points × radii per pair (at least 5 radii per pair) |
| **Refined iterations** | 0–100 000 | 500 | Additional surfaces around the best ones |

More iterations give a more thorough search and take longer. For a final analysis, check that doubling the iterations no longer lowers the factor of safety noticeably.

## Analysis direction

**Sliding direction** in **Options › Search** sets whether the slope fails **Left to right** or **Right to left**. A DXF import guesses the direction from the geometry. Choose **Both** to search both: the opposite direction uses automatic search limits, and the lower factor of safety is reported, with the other one in the summary.

**Circles follow bedrock (composite)** is on by default. A trial circle that reaches impenetrable material (bedrock) on the base of the model follows its top there instead of being rejected: the slip surface runs along the firm base, as for clay on a firm bottom, and the slices along it have a straight base whose forces take their moments about the circle's center (Duncan & Wright 2014, ch. 7, composite surfaces). The result shows such a surface as a circle with a straight part along the base. Turn it off to analyze circles only, as programs without composite surfaces do.

**…and the base of the model** (off by default) lets the circles follow the base of the model too, where no impenetrable material lies on it, as if the material below the model were impenetrable. Use it when the base of the model is a firm stratum, not just where the drawing ends; imported .gsz files turn it on, since their circles follow the bottom of the geometry. On the James Bay dyke, whose till is the base of the model, the circles alone give 1.45 and the composite surfaces 1.17, the value Duncan & Wright give for the composite surface.

## Shallow surfaces

Very shallow surfaces at the ground surface can give a low factor of safety that does not matter for the design, especially in frictional soil. Three settings in the options leave them out:

- **Min. slip depth (m)**: surfaces whose greatest depth below the ground is less than this are not analyzed. 0 means no limit.
- **Min. slip area (m²)**: surfaces whose sliding mass (cross-section area per meter) is smaller than this are left out of the search. 0 means no limit, the default. A limit hides small surfaces, such as a low cut at the toe; use it to skip shallow slips you do not want to analyze.
- **Min. entry–exit distance (m)**: surfaces whose entry and exit points are closer than this (the straight distance between them; horizontally for planar surfaces) are not analyzed. 0 means no limit. In a cohesionless fill (a road bank's 45° edge, a loose fill over clay) the search otherwise finds a thin slide in the fill before any deeper mechanism; when the critical surface is such a slide (its base on average less than 1 m, or a tenth of the ground relief around it, below the ground, with half its base or more in cohesionless soil), the result warns and points to this setting, the minimum depth and the minimum area.

## Non-circular surfaces

Tick **Non-circular optimization** in the options to let cSlope refine the best circles into non-circular surfaces. It moves the points of each surface to lower the factor of safety further. Use it where a weak layer or a stiff base makes a non-circular failure likely. The optimized surface is shown in the results like any other.

## Block search

Where the failure is expected to run along a weak layer or a stiff base, a **block search** tries surfaces through regions you choose instead of circles. Set **Search method** to **Block (through search objects)** in the options and add **search objects** below it, each as points `x, y; x, y; …`:

- **one point** is a fixed point every surface passes through,
- **two points** are a line: each surface passes through a point on it,
- **three or more points** are a window (a polygon): each surface passes through a point inside it.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/block-search/01-options.png" alt="The Options window with the block search, two lines and angle ranges" caption="Block search in the options: two lines and the angle ranges of the end segments." />

Each trial surface takes one point from every object, joins them from left to right as the base of the sliding block, and rises from both ends of the base to the ground. The upslope end rises at the **upslope end angle**, the downslope end at the **downslope end angle**. Left empty, these are the Rankine angles of the soil the end segments cut: 45° + φ/2 upslope (active) and 45° − φ/2 downslope (passive). Give a range, for example 45–70, to let the search try angles within it.

The objects are drawn on the canvas, numbered B1, B2, … as in the options. **Iterations** is the number of block surfaces tried; **Refined iterations** adds surfaces around the best ones, in neighborhoods that shrink step by step. The draws are repeatable: the same model gives the same result. The search limits apply to block surfaces as to circles. Tick **Non-circular optimization** to optimize the best block surfaces further; the optimized surfaces may leave the objects.

A typical block search for a weak layer uses two lines or two thin windows along the layer: one under the crest, one under the toe.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/block-search/02-objects.png" alt="Two block search lines, B1 and B2, along the base of a weak layer" caption="Two lines, B1 and B2, along the base of the weak layer of Arai & Tagyo's example." />

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/block-search/03-result.png" alt="The block surfaces analyzed and the critical one along the weak layer" caption="The block surfaces analyzed, colored by factor of safety, and the critical one along the layer (0.450; 0.414 with Non-circular optimization, the published value)." />

The critical surface often has flatter end segments than the Rankine angles, especially in cohesive soil: on this example the Rankine angles give 0.538, ranges of 25–70° upslope and 5–45° downslope 0.450. Give ranges, or tick **Non-circular optimization**, for a final analysis.

## Custom surfaces

Open **Model › Analysis › Custom planes** to analyze surfaces of your own:

- **+ Add circle** adds a circle, given by its center (cx, cy) and radius R.
- **+ Add non-circular surface** adds a polyline through the soil. Enter its points as `x, y; x, y; …` with x increasing, for example `10, 5; 18, 1; 30, 2; 38, 9`. The ends are extended to the ground.

Custom surfaces are analyzed together with the search. Tick **Only analyze these surfaces (skip the search)** to analyze only your own surfaces, for example to check a published case or a surface found in the field.

The result summary lists them as *Custom surface 1*, *2*, …, the circles first and then the polylines, each with its factor of safety or the reason it has none. **Results › Display › Surfaces › Custom surfaces** draws them on the sheet (see [Results](/docs/results#the-summary)).

## Surfaces that are not analyzed

A surface is skipped when it:

- enters bedrock (Impenetrable) or has a slice base in high strength material,
- has no driving force in the sliding direction,
- does not reach equilibrium,
- is shallower than **Min. slip depth** or smaller than **Min. slip area** (surfaces shallower than 0.1 % of the model height are always skipped),
- gives a negative factor of safety, or
- has a slice with $m_\alpha \le 0$ (see [Bishop simplified](/docs/theory#bishop-simplified)), where the normal force on the base has no meaning.

The result summary counts the surfaces that were discarded because they did not reach equilibrium.
