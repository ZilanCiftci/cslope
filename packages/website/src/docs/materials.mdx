Open **Model › Materials**. The table at the top lists the materials with their name, model and colour. **Add** creates a material; **Delete** removes the selected one (the last material cannot be deleted). Select a material to edit its properties below the table.

Every numeric property accepts a number or an expression with [parameters](/docs/geometry#parameters), and a comma as the decimal separator. Problems with the input are listed in red under the form.

## Material models

Choose the model in the **Material Model** list. Changing the model resets the strength properties to that model's defaults and keeps the unit weights.

| Model | Properties | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mohr-Coulomb** | γ (kN/m³), φ (°), c (kPa) | Drained analyses with effective strength parameters. |
| **Undrained** | γ, Su (kPa), Max Su (cutoff) | Clay with a constant undrained shear strength. |
| **S = f(depth)** | γ, Su ref, Depth ref, Rate (kPa/m), Depth measured from, Max Su | Undrained strength increasing with depth below the ground or below the top of the layer. |
| **S = f(datum)** | γ, Su ref, Y ref, Rate, Max Su | Undrained strength that varies with elevation. |
| **Anisotropic Function** | γ, φ, c, modifier table | Strength that depends on the inclination of the slip surface. |
| **Spatial Mohr-Coulomb** | γ, interpolation, data points | Strength that varies from point to point in the section. |
| **Lime-cement columns** | γ, area ratio, soil and column strengths | Ground improved with lime-cement columns. |
| **High Strength** | γ | Strong material that may lie in the sliding mass; slip surfaces may not run through it. |
| **Impenetrable (Bedrock)** | γ | Material slip surfaces cannot enter. |

For the undrained models the friction angle is zero and the strength at the base of each slice is the undrained shear strength $S_u$.

### Mohr-Coulomb

The shear strength is $\tau = c' + \sigma'_n \tan\varphi'$, with the effective normal stress from the slice weight and the pore pressure. A new material is Mohr-Coulomb with γ = 20 kN/m³, c = 10 kPa and φ = 30°.

### Undrained

A constant undrained shear strength **Su**.

### S = f(depth)

$$
S_u = S_{u,\text{ref}} + (z - z_\text{ref}) \cdot \text{rate}
$$

where $z$ is the depth of the point and $z_\text{ref}$ the **Depth ref**. **Depth measured from** chooses where the depth starts:

- **Top of layer** (default): the depth below the top of this material's layer above the slice. A layer that lies below other layers then starts at Su ref at its own top.
- **Ground surface**: the depth below the ground surface.

### S = f(datum)

$$
S_u = S_{u,\text{ref}} + (Y_\text{ref} - y) \cdot \text{rate}
$$

where $y$ is the elevation of the point and $Y_\text{ref}$ the datum. A positive rate makes $S_u$ increase with depth below the datum.

### Strength cutoff

The undrained models and lime-cement columns have a **Max Su (cutoff)**. Leave it empty for no cutoff. When the strength is constant or increases with depth, the cutoff is a maximum; when the rate is negative, it is a minimum, so that the strength never falls below it.

### Anisotropic Function

Mohr-Coulomb strength in which c and φ are both multiplied by a factor that depends on the inclination α of the slice base, measured relative to the sliding direction. Enter the factors as a table of α (°) and factor; cSlope interpolates between the rows.

### Spatial Mohr-Coulomb

c, φ and γ given at data points (X, Y). Between the points cSlope interpolates by **IDW** (inverse distance weighting, power 2), **Linear** (inverse distance weighting, power 1) or **Nearest** (the value of the nearest point).

### Lime-cement columns

The strength of the improved ground is the area-weighted strength of the columns and the soil between them:

$$
\tau = a\,\tau_\text{col} + (1 - a)\,c_{u,\text{soil}}
$$

where **Area ratio a** is the share of the area taken by columns (0–1). The soil strength is **Su soil**, increasing by **Su soil increase** per metre below **Su soil at level**; the column strength is **Su columns**. Tick **Drained limit of the column strength** to also limit the column strength by drained parameters **c′ columns** and **φ′ columns**. The cutoff applies to the soil between the columns only. With [partial factors](/docs/analysis#partial-factors), the strength of soil and columns (including c′ columns) is divided by γcu and tan φ′ columns by γφ′.

### High Strength and Bedrock

**High Strength** material is so strong that the slip surface cannot run through it: a surface with a slice base in it is not analysed. The material can lie inside the sliding mass, above the slip surface, where it adds its weight. **Impenetrable (Bedrock)** stops slip surfaces: a surface that would enter it is not analysed. Both are drawn with a hatch on the canvas (HS and BR).

## Saturated unit weight

Every model except Bedrock has a **γsat below water** field. Below the material's piezometric line, the soil then weighs γsat instead of γ. Leave it empty to use γ throughout.

## Colours

Each material has a colour, used for its regions on the canvas, in the material table and in exports. Click the colour in the table to change it. **Soil colors** on the Results tab hides the colours in the result view.
