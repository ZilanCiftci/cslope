Open **Model › Materials**. The table at the top lists the materials with their name, model and colour. **Add** creates a material; **Delete** removes the selected one (the last material cannot be deleted). Select a material to edit its properties below the table.

Every numeric property accepts a number or an expression with [parameters](/docs/geometry#parameters), and a comma as the decimal separator. Problems with the input are listed in red under the form.

## Material models

Choose the model in the **Material Model** list. Changing the model resets the strength properties to that model's defaults and keeps the unit weights.

| Model | Properties | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mohr-Coulomb** | γ (kN/m³), φ (°), c (kPa), φb, Max suction | Drained analyses with effective strength parameters. |
| **Undrained** | γ, Su (kPa), Max Su (cutoff) | Clay with a constant undrained shear strength. |
| **S = f(depth)** | γ, Su ref, Depth ref, Rate (kPa/m), Depth measured from, Max Su; combined: c′, c′ rate, φ′, φb, Max suction | Undrained strength increasing with depth below the ground or below the top of the layer, alone or combined with a drained strength. |
| **S = f(datum)** | γ, Su ref, Y ref, Rate, Max Su | Undrained strength that varies with elevation. |
| **Anisotropic Function** | γ, φ, c, φb, Max suction, modifier table | Strength that depends on the inclination of the slip surface. |
| **Spatial Mohr-Coulomb** | γ, interpolation, data points | Strength that varies from point to point in the section. |
| **Lime-cement columns** | γ, area ratio, soil and column strengths | Ground improved with lime-cement columns. |
| **SHANSEP** | γ, S, m, OCR or OCR against elevation, or Su against σ′v, Min Su | Undrained strength from the effective vertical stress and the stress history. |
| **Shear-normal function** | γ, points (σ′n, τ) | A curved strength envelope given as points. |
| **High Strength** | γ | Strong material that may lie in the sliding mass; slip surfaces may not run through it. |
| **Impenetrable (Bedrock)** | γ | Material slip surfaces cannot enter. |

For the undrained models the friction angle is zero and the strength at the base of each slice is the undrained shear strength $S_u$.

### Mohr-Coulomb

The shear strength is $\tau = c' + \sigma'_n \tan\varphi'$, with the effective normal stress from the slice weight and the pore pressure. A new material is Mohr-Coulomb with γ = 20 kN/m³, c = 10 kPa and φ = 30°.

### Undrained

A constant undrained shear strength **Su**.

### S = f(depth)

$$
S_u = S_{u,\text{ref}} + (z - z_\text{ref}) \cdot \text{rate}
$$

where $z$ is the depth of the point and $z_\text{ref}$ the **Depth ref**. **Depth measured from** chooses where the depth starts:

- **Top of layer** (default): the depth below the top of this material's layer above the slice. A layer that lies below other layers then starts at Su ref at its own top.
- **Ground surface**: the depth below the ground surface.

**Combined analysis (drained limit)** adds a drained strength, for clay in a combined analysis. The strength at the base of a slice is then the lesser of the two:

$$
\tau = \min\left(S_u,\; c' + \sigma'_n \tan\varphi'\right), \qquad c' = c'_\text{ref} + (z - z_\text{ref}) \cdot \text{rate}_{c'}
$$

with $c'$ at the same depth $z$ as $S_u$. Ticking the box fills in c′ = 0.1·Su and φ′ = 30° as a starting point. With partial factors, $S_u$ takes γcu and $c'$ and $\tan\varphi'$ their own factors. The anisotropy applies to $S_u$ only. In the SGI 3D analysis of a temporary slope the end faces take $S_u$.

### S = f(datum)

$$
S_u = S_{u,\text{ref}} + (Y_\text{ref} - y) \cdot \text{rate}
$$

where $y$ is the elevation of the point and $Y_\text{ref}$ the datum. A positive rate makes $S_u$ increase with depth below the datum.

### Strength cutoff

The undrained models and lime-cement columns have a **Max Su (cutoff)**. Leave it empty for no cutoff. When the strength is constant or increases with depth, the cutoff is a maximum; when the rate is negative, it is a minimum, so that the strength never falls below it.

### Anisotropic Function

Mohr-Coulomb strength in which c and φ are both multiplied by a factor that depends on the inclination α of the slice base, measured relative to the sliding direction. Enter the factors as a table of α (°) and factor; cSlope interpolates between the rows.

### Spatial Mohr-Coulomb

c, φ and γ given at data points (X, Y). Between the points cSlope interpolates by **IDW** (inverse distance weighting, power 2), **Linear** (inverse distance weighting, power 1) or **Nearest** (the value of the nearest point).

### Lime-cement columns

The strength of the improved ground is the area-weighted strength of the columns and the soil between them:

$$
\tau = a\,\tau_\text{col} + (1 - a)\,c_{u,\text{soil}}
$$

where **Area ratio a** is the share of the area taken by columns (0–1). The soil strength is **Su soil**, increasing by **Su soil increase** per metre below **Su soil at level**; the column strength is **Su columns**. Tick **Drained limit of the column strength** to also limit the column strength by drained parameters **c′ columns** and **φ′ columns**. The cutoff applies to the soil between the columns only. With [partial factors](/docs/analysis#partial-factors), each part takes its own factor: the undrained strength of soil and columns is divided by γcu, c′ columns by γc' and tan φ′ columns by γφ′.

### SHANSEP

The undrained strength at the base of each slice follows from the effective vertical stress there and the stress history (Ladd & Foott 1974):

$$
S_u = \sigma'_v \, S \, \mathrm{OCR}^m
$$

**S** is $S_u/\sigma'_v$ of the normally consolidated soil and **m** the exponent (often 0.8). **OCR** is a constant or a table against elevation; alternatively **Su against σ′v** gives the strength directly from the stress. $\sigma'_v$ is the weight of the slice and the loads on it per metre of width less the pore pressure at its base, so a fill on the clay raises the strength under it and a higher water table lowers it. **Min Su** is a lower limit. The tables are interpolated linearly, or with a cubic spline when the model is imported with one.

Instead of OCR the stress history can be a **POP** (pre-overburden pressure, $\mathrm{OCR} = (\sigma'_v + \mathrm{POP})/\sigma'_v$) or a **yield stress** ($\mathrm{OCR} = \sigma'_y/\sigma'_v$, at least 1). With an earlier stage (imported from .stix files), the yield stress is the effective vertical stress of that stage at the base plus the POP: the stress history the soil kept from it. An imported POP, yield stress or earlier stage overrides OCR: the OCR fields are then disabled, with a note such as "Overridden by imported POP 20 kPa", and **Use OCR instead** clears the stress history so that the OCR fields apply (one step to undo). The Define Materials window says when a model has an earlier stage and which materials take their yield stress from it (marked "stage" in the material list); **Remove earlier stage** drops it, and those materials then use their POP or OCR.

A material can have its own **strength above its water line** (for example drained above and undrained below the phreatic level); it applies to slice bases above the line, and everywhere when the material has no line. It is imported from .stix files. The Define Materials window shows it read-only (its model and main values) under the fields, which set the strength below the line; **Remove** drops it, so that one strength applies throughout.

### Shear-normal function

The shear strength against the effective normal stress, $\tau = f(\sigma'_n)$, as points; linear between them or a cubic spline, the end segments extended. Each slice base takes the tangent c and φ of the curve at its normal stress, which the analysis updates from the normal stress it finds until the factor of safety settles, as for Hoek-Brown.

### Pore pressure ratio (rᵤ)

**rᵤ** on a material sets the pore pressure at a slice base in it to rᵤ times the weight of the soil above the base per metre of width, $u = r_u\,\gamma h$ (Bishop & Morgenstern 1960), added to the pore pressure of its water line if it has one. It is a simple way to describe pore pressure that rises with depth, used in stability charts and in older analyses. 0 ≤ rᵤ < 1; a value outside this range is kept, the field is outlined red and the error shows under it (as for B̄, 0 to 1.5). The finite element analyses do not take it. rᵤ and B̄ are under the heading *Additional pore pressure (added to the water line)*.

### Pore pressure from a fill (B̄)

**B̄** on a material raises the pore pressure at a slice base in it by $\bar B$ times the vertical stress of the fills above it: the materials ticked **This material is a fill that loads the B̄ materials below**. This is the short-term pore pressure of a soft foundation under a new embankment (Skempton 1954). It adds to the pore pressure of the material's water line. The finite element analyses do not take B̄ either.

### Drawdown envelope (rapid drawdown)

A Mohr-Coulomb material can have a **drawdown strength (R envelope, cR, φR)**: the total-stress envelope of isotropically consolidated undrained tests. In a [rapid drawdown analysis](/docs/water#rapid-drawdown) such a soil does not drain; without the envelope it is free-draining.

### High Strength and Bedrock

**High Strength** material is so strong that the slip surface cannot run through it: a surface with a slice base in it is not analysed. The material can lie inside the sliding mass, above the slip surface, where it adds its weight. **Impenetrable (Bedrock)** stops slip surfaces: a surface that would enter it is not analysed. Both are drawn with a hatch on the canvas (HS and BR).

## Strength from suction

Above the water table the soil is unsaturated and the negative pore pressure (matric suction) adds to its strength. **φb, suction (°)** takes this into account with the extended Mohr-Coulomb criterion of Fredlund, Morgenstern and Widger (1978), with the pore-air pressure $u_a = 0$:

$$
\tau = c' + \sigma_n \tan\varphi' + (u_a - u_w) \tan\varphi^b
$$

Above the material's [piezometric line](/docs/water#pore-pressure) the pore pressure is negative, $u = -\gamma_w\, h$, with $h$ the head measured as below the line (the vertical distance up from the line, or with the phreatic correction the vertical distance × cos²θ). The suction strength $(-u) \tan\varphi^b$ is added to c′ on the slice like an apparent cohesion, and the pore pressure on the base stays zero. Below the line nothing changes, and without a piezometric line there is no suction. **Max suction (kPa)**, shown once φb is set, limits the suction taken into account; leave it empty for no limit. φb is typically 10–20°, at most φ′.

- φb is taken by the drained strength: Mohr-Coulomb (with a c-factor, its drained branch), the Anisotropic Function (the suction strength is not multiplied by the modifier) and the drained branch of a combined S = f(depth). The undrained strengths, Spatial Mohr-Coulomb, lime-cement columns and Hoek-Brown do not have it.
- With [partial factors](/docs/analysis#partial-factors) the suction strength takes γc' like c′, also on a Mohr-Coulomb material with φ = 0.
- The pore pressure in the slice table and the slice plots is negative above the line on bases in a material with φb.
- The finite element analyses take it at each Gauss point, with the vertical head above the line (or the negative pore pressure of the seepage with pore pressure from seepage). The Rankine depth of an automatic tension crack and the Ito & Matsui pile pressure do not take it.

## Saturated unit weight

Every model except Bedrock has a **γsat below water** field. Below the material's piezometric line, the soil then weighs γsat instead of γ. Leave it empty to use γ throughout.

## Colours

Each material has a colour, used for its regions on the canvas, in the material table and in exports. Click the colour in the table to change it. **Soil colors** on the Results tab hides the colours in the result view.
