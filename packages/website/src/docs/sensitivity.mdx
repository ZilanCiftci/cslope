Both analyses build on a finished model. Open them from **Results › Plots**.

## Sensitivity analysis

**Sensitivity** runs the complete analysis, including the search, for a range of values of one input and plots the factor of safety against it.

1. Under **Vary**, choose the input: any [parameter](/docs/geometry#parameters), or a property of a material (unit weight, c, φ, Su and so on). The list shows each input's current value.
2. Set **From** and **To**. They start at ±20 % of the current value.
3. Set **Values**, the number of analyses between them (2–101, default 9).
4. Click **Run**.

<Figure src="/media/tutorials/probabilistic/01-sensitivity.png" alt="The sensitivity panel with a chart of the factor of safety against unit weight" caption="A sensitivity analysis of the unit weight." />

The chart shows the factor of safety against the value, with a line at 1.0. The table lists the values. **Export CSV** saves them (also with `;` as the separator and `,` as the decimal mark, for spreadsheets in many European locales).

<Callout type="tip">
  To vary a dimension, such as the height of an embankment or the depth of an excavation, define it as a parameter and use the parameter in the coordinates. The sensitivity analysis can then vary it like any other input.
</Callout>

## Probabilistic analysis

**Probabilistic** treats material properties as random variables and computes the probability that the factor of safety is below 1.

### Variables

Click **+ Add variable** and choose a material property. Its mean is the value in the model. Enter its standard deviation **σ** (the default corresponds to a coefficient of variation of 5 % for unit weights, 10 % for friction angles and 25 % for other properties) and its **Distribution**, normal or lognormal.

### Methods

- **Taylor series**: the factor of safety at the mean values and with each variable at its mean ± σ, 1 + 2n analyses for n variables. With **Search the critical surface again for each value** (default) each analysis is a full search; otherwise the critical surface at the mean values is reused.
- **Monte Carlo**: one search at the mean values, then the given number of **Samples** (default 2000) analysed on that critical surface. The **Seed** makes the samples reproducible. Normal samples are truncated at zero.

### Results

- the factor of safety at the mean values (and the mean of the samples for Monte Carlo),
- the standard deviation and coefficient of variation of the factor of safety,
- the reliability index β and the probability of failure, assuming a lognormal distribution of the factor of safety,
- for Monte Carlo, the share of samples with a factor of safety below 1 and a histogram of the samples,
- for the Taylor series, a table of the factor of safety at mean + σ and mean − σ of each variable, which shows which variables matter most.

The results can be exported as CSV. Sensitivity and probabilistic results are not saved with the project; run them again after opening it. The equations are on the [theory](/docs/theory#reliability) page.
