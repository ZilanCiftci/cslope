Pore pressures in cSlope come from **piezometric lines**. Open **Model › Water & parameters › Piezometric lines**.

## Adding a line

**Add** creates a horizontal line across the model at mid-height, named *Line 1*, *Line 2* and so on. Select a line in the list to edit its points in the table below, or drag its points on the canvas. Right-click a line on the canvas to add a point, or a point to delete it. A line keeps at least two points. **Delete** removes the selected line.

Water is part of the analysis as soon as one line exists. Without a line the analysis is dry.

## Pore pressure

At a point below a piezometric line, the pore pressure is

$$
u = \gamma_w\, h
$$

with $\gamma_w = 9.81\ \text{kN/m}^3$ and $h$ the pressure head. Above the line the pore pressure is zero, except in a material with [strength from suction](/docs/materials#strength-from-suction) (φb), where it is negative, $u = -\gamma_w\, h$, with $h$ measured the same way up from the line.

How $h$ is measured is set in **Model › Analysis › Options**, under *Pore pressure below an inclined water line*:

- **Vertical head (conventional)**, the default: the vertical distance from the point up to the line.
- **Phreatic correction (vertical head × cos²θ)**: the vertical distance multiplied by cos²θ, where θ is the inclination of the line. This assumes a free phreatic surface with flow parallel to it and gives lower pore pressures below a steep line.

## Several lines

With two or more lines, the window shows two more settings:

- **Main line**: the line that gives the pore pressure, and the free water above the ground, of materials without a line of their own. By default the first line.
- **Material Assignment**: one line per material. Use it for perched water tables or a separate pressure level in an aquifer below a clay layer.

A material's line is also the level below which its [saturated unit weight](/docs/materials#saturated-unit-weight) applies.

## Head and reference lines (waternet)

A model imported from a .stix file can take its pore pressure from head lines and reference lines. Between two reference lines the head varies linearly with elevation, from the bottom head line of the upper line to the top head line of the lower one; below the lowest line it is that line's bottom head; above the uppermost line it varies linearly from that line's top head to the phreatic level, and above the phreatic line there is no pore pressure. A reference line without head lines is left out. This describes, for example, a clay layer between the phreatic level and an aquifer under a higher head. The pore pressure is $\gamma_w (h - z)$, not negative. The piezometric lines window says when such lines set the pore pressure. The piezometric lines still set the phreatic level then: the saturated unit weight below it, the free water on the ground and the strength above the water line; only the pore pressure comes from the head lines. **Remove head lines** drops the head and reference lines, and the piezometric lines then set the pore pressure too (Undo brings them back). The edit canvas draws the head lines teal and dash-dotted, labelled "head line 1", "head line 2" and so on at their right end, and the reference lines faintly dotted. A .stix stage without a phreatic line takes its phreatic level from the top head line of its uppermost reference line (else its first head line); the import notes say so.

## Free water above the ground

**Free water above ground (ponding)** is on by default. Where the line of the material at the ground (its own line, else the main line) lies above the ground surface, for example at the toe of a slope in a river or a reservoir, the water stands on the slope. Its weight acts on the slices below it, and against a vertical face it pushes horizontally. This is the usual way to model a submerged toe or a full reservoir.

With the box unticked, the lines are clipped to the ground surface and no water stands on the slope.

<Callout type="note">
  A fully submerged slope gives the same factor of safety as the same slope in the dry with buoyant unit weights. This is one of the checks in the [verification](/verification).
</Callout>

## Rapid drawdown

With **Rapid drawdown** on, the model's water is the state after drawdown and **Water before drawdown** gives the water table before it, as a level across the model. A line imported with the model is shown as "Imported line (N points)" and kept until you click **Replace with level**, which starts the level at the line's highest point. Materials with a [drawdown envelope](/docs/materials#drawdown-envelope-rapid-drawdown) do not drain; the analysis follows the three-stage procedure of Duncan, Wright & Wong (1990):

1. Before drawdown, with the drained strengths and the water (and reservoir) before it, the effective normal stress $\sigma'_{fc}$ and the shear stress $\tau_{fc}$ on each slice base.
2. After drawdown, the undrained strength $\tau_{ff}$ at those consolidation stresses, interpolated by the consolidation ratio $K_c$ between the drawdown envelope ($K_c = 1$) and the effective envelope ($K_c = K_f$).
3. Where the drained strength with the water after drawdown is lower than $\tau_{ff}$, the drained strength.

Free-draining materials take their drained strength with the water after drawdown.

## Water in a tension crack

A tension crack can be partly or fully filled with water. The water pushes on the sliding mass. Set it with *Water in crack (%)* in the analysis options; see [Tension cracks](/docs/analysis#tension-crack).
