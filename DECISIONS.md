# Decisions

A log of the decisions behind cSlope's behavior and look, newest first, so that later work knows why something is the way it is. One entry per decision: the date, what was decided, the alternatives that were turned down, and where it is implemented. The rules that follow from them live in [docs/design-rules.md](docs/design-rules.md) and [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md); this file keeps the reasons.

Add an entry when the maintainer chooses between options (an issue labeled `needs-decision`, a design review), in the same PR as the change.

## Engine

### 2026-11-10: Rust is the only engine; no bit identity with the removed TypeScript engine

The engine is optimised as Rust code in its own right. A change must keep the verification checks against the golden values (F within 1e-6) and keep native, WebAssembly and Python bit-identical to each other; it need not reproduce the TypeScript engine's arithmetic. Turned down: keeping TypeScript's operation order everywhere, which blocked restructuring the slice builder and the solvers.

## Process

### 2026-11-06: Work flows through issues and sprints

Bugs and wishes are GitHub issues; the maintainer picks a sprint's issues (`sprint: <n>` label); agents build them in groups, one PR per group, which merge on their own after the verify gate and CI. See [.claude/workflow.md](.claude/workflow.md). While in beta, fixes merge without a human review; **revisit before the first non-beta release** (then PRs wait for approval).

### 2026-11-06: The website deploys on every merge to main

Vercel builds only main; "skip deployments when there are no changes" is off, because the site reads files outside its folder (CHANGELOG.md, the app's version). Vercel does not comment on pull requests.

### 2026-11: Pull requests with auto-merge; main protected

Every change goes through a PR with auto-merge (squash); main requires the `check` and `desktop` checks and an up-to-date branch, no approvals. The repository is LF throughout (`.gitattributes`).

## Import and export

### 2026-11-11: The .gsz export writes a water line as drawn, also when free water is off (issue #149)

In the .gsz format a piezometric line above the ground is water standing on it; the format has no setting that turns this off (the public example files carry only display settings for it). A cSlope model with free water off (its water lines clipped to the ground, D-24) and a line above the ground therefore gives another factor of safety from the exported file: on a synthetic slope 2.6317 in cSlope against 2.6428 from the file, on the same circle. The export keeps the line as the user drew it and says so in the export message ("The water lines are clipped to the ground in cSlope; the format carries free water above the ground where a line is above it."). With free water on, which is the app's default, or with the line below the ground, export and re-import give the same factor of safety. Turned down: writing the line clipped to the ground when free water is off (the file would give the same factor, 2.6317 on that slope, but the line in the file would no longer be the one drawn, and the clipping needs rules for vertical faces, overhangs and materials with lines of their own). The behaviour is pinned by gsz-round-trip.test.ts.

## Engine

### 2026-11-12: The first pass puts points on the break points of the ground (issue #164)

In each entry and exit range whose ground is not one straight line, the vertices where the ground turns by more than 30° (the crest, the toe, the corners of a berm or a wall) are break points, in an exit range also a toe that turns by more than 15° (the ground bends upward there and runs on within 10° of level beyond it), and the first pass's inner point nearest to each is moved onto it, the sharpest break first and each point at most once (variant a: the number of points and the other points' places are unchanged). At most half of a range's points are moved, so a surveyed ground with many vertices keeps its sharpest breaks and the rest of its points evenly spaced; the ends of a range stay at its limits. In an exit range the point stands 1e-5 of the model's width up the ground from the break, as the corner rule of `search_limits` puts an exit at the toe (a circle exactly through the toe can touch it and run on in the soil); at the foot of a vertical face that is the point `exit_face_foot` gives. The refinement and the local search are unchanged; a side given as a point (#157) and an exit range ending at a wall face (#158) keep their rules. Measured first (step 0): in the anisotropy test's model the critical circle exits exactly at the toe and the nearest first-pass exit lay 1.3 m from it (1.6 % too high at 1 000 iterations); V059b's rise in #158 is not at a break point (its 1.2892 surface enters at the model's left end, a point of both first passes, and exits on the floor 16 m beyond the toe: which circles seed the non-circular optimisation changed), and it stays at 1.3118. Measured (2026-11-12, against main): on 37 imported example models the mean distance above the best F known goes from 0.037 to 0.024 % (0.0355 % after #160), 25 with a point limit unchanged at 0.024 %, run time unchanged within the noise; 99 of 208 verification searches move, three by more than 0.1 % (V026 Morgenstern-Price at 1 000 iterations 1.19546 → 1.16454, published 1.19, tolerance 2.42 %; V104a Bishop 2.29997 → 2.29191; V028 Bishop 1.12464 → 1.12337), none leaves its tolerance. V026's value falls further below Bishop (1.2203): accepted, decided by the maintainer, because non-circular searches of the problem come out as low (main 1.1703, 1.1648 with another variant of this first pass), and it stays within the tolerance; no other Morgenstern-Price result falls further below Bishop. The anisotropy test runs again at 300 iterations (0.85 % between its two models; 3.2 % without the toe point). Variants compared with the same harness (gap on the 37 models; verification moves): the break points added on top of the count (c, 0.036 %, 18 % more surfaces and up to 30 % more time on the verification set, V038 Morgenstern-Price +1.2 %), the points spread proportionally over the stretches between the breaks (b, 0.044 %), all three with the break exactly on the corner (0.036–0.044 %; the offset above it gives most of the gain). The thresholds: the maintainer decided that the toes of 2:1 and 3:1 slopes (26.6° and 18.4°) get a point. One threshold of 10° or 15° for every break gave a gap of 0.021 or 0.023 %, but crests of 21.8° moved V038's Morgenstern-Price search up by 1.45 % (to 1.1000, published 1.10, against 1.0778 that main finds at 8 000 iterations: a miss on the unsafe side) and the 26.6° toe in V034a's entry range moved its non-circular Morgenstern-Price search by −0.24 %, further below Bishop. Separate thresholds by range did not avoid it: entry 15° / exit 30° V038 +1.20 %, entry 30° / exit 15° V038 +0.61 %. A toe rule did: 30° for every break, and from 15° the toes in exit ranges (gap 0.024 %, V038 and V034a's non-circular search unmoved); the same rule in entry ranges too, or every concave break from 15°, moved V034a again. With the toe rule in exit ranges the toe of a slope as flat as about 3.7:1 (15.1°) gets its point. The 10° limit of level ground beyond a toe is a judgement: the measurements did not separate it from other values, as the toes in the verification problems and the imported models have level ground beyond them. At 40° for every break fewer models gain (0.027 %), at 50° the toe of a 1:1 slope is left out and nothing changes. One synthetic pin rises: an exit range across a 10 m wall face, 0.392545 → 0.394089 (+0.39 %; its critical surface is nearly planar, radius 341 m, and the search now ends on a 123 m circle through the same ends). Decided by the maintainer (fixed points along the ground; the toes of 2:1 and 3:1 slopes; V026 accepted); variant and rule chosen by these measurements. Implemented in `GroundPath::first_pass_points` (model/search.rs).

### 2026-11-12: The search's entry and exit points are spaced evenly along the ground (issue #158)

The first pass places the entry and exit points of each range at equal distances along the ground between its two limits (arc length, across slope breaks; a vertical face inside a range gets points on it), and the refinement's half steps and the local search's steps are taken along the ground too (variant b). The limits stay x values. Where the ground of a range is one straight line the points are those spaced evenly in x, bit for bit. A vertical face at a limit's x: an end of an exit range on a face that descends in the sliding direction (and is not an end of the model) is the foot of the face, moved up it by 1e-5 of the model's width, as a single exit there has been since #141/#147. An exit range that starts at such a face starts at its foot (the corner rule of `search_limits` no longer moves that end onto the crest), and one that ends at it runs down the face to its foot; any other face at a limit belongs to the range whole, and an entry range takes the ground as it is. On a synthetic 10 m wall the search from the face to 10 m beyond it gives 0.394089 and the one from 3 m behind the crest to the face 0.394092, against 0.394092 for the single exit at the foot. Measured together with #157 and #160 (2026-11-12, against main): on 37 imported example models the mean distance above the best F known is 0.037 % (0.065 % before), on 25 with a point limit 0.024 % (0.114 %), with 7 % less run time; 205 of 208 verification searches move, none leaves its tolerance, three by more than 0.3 %: V059b Spencer non-circular 1.2892 → 1.3118 (+1.76 %, published 1.29, tolerance 2.39 %), V038 Morgenstern-Price 1.0786 → 1.0843 (towards the published 1.10) and V104a Bishop 2.2921 → 2.3000 (towards 2.30). On its own the change did not improve the search (measured first: 0.081 % against 0.065 %); with #160 it did on the point-limit models. Turned down: the first pass along the ground with the refinement and the local search in x (variant a, slightly worse); keeping the corner rule for an exit range starting at a wall face (it put the range's first exits on the crest, which #147 turned down for a single exit). The desktop still draws the marker of such a range end on the ground at x, not at the foot. Decided by the maintainer; implemented in `GroundPath` (model/search.rs) and `Slope::exit_face_foot`.

### 2026-11-12: The first pass tries 11 radii from 5 % to 95 % of the reference depth on 9 × 11 pairs (issue #160)

Through each pair of an entry and an exit point the first pass tries 11 circles whose sagittas are spaced evenly from 95 % to 5 % of the reference depth D (the sagitta of the circle through both points whose centre is at the elevation of the higher one, radius half-chord / cos β), on 9 entry × 11 exit points at 1 000 iterations (1 089 circles), the point counts scaled with the square root of the iterations (18 × 22 at 4 000). Before: 5 sagittas, k/5 of the start circle's (radius 1.1 × half-chord / cos β), on 14 × 16 pairs. The circles touching the base and the material interfaces are found as before; the refinement and the local search keep their number of circles per pair and step by the new point spacing; the first pass stays at 1 000 iterations. Measured (2026-11-12, with #157, against main): on 37 imported example models the mean distance above the best F known goes from 0.064 to 0.036 % (largest 0.63 → 0.17 %), on 25 with a point limit from 0.096 to 0.024 %, with 7 % less run time; 205 of 208 verification searches move, 10 by more than 0.1 %, one by more than 0.5 % (V038 Morgenstern-Price, towards the published value), none leaves its tolerance, and no Morgenstern-Price result falls further below the other methods of its problem. Verification log: against the programs mean absolute error 0.308 → 0.311 %, RMS 0.441 → 0.443 %; against the papers 0.951 → 0.949 %, RMS 1.536 → 1.537 %. Two defects of the search came to light and were fixed first, each with a test: the local search dropped the unmoved end of a seed that lay a few ulps outside a limit (a seed at the corner-widened toe then had no candidates, the empty level ended the local search, and two imported models ended 0.2 % high after 33 of 500 refinement surfaces); and a pair given right to left (exit left of entry, where the ranges overlap) had no circle tangent to a material interface. At low budgets the coarser pairs show: at 400 iterations (6 × 7 pairs) a synthetic embankment ends 1.1 % above its converged F (0.25 % before), at 1 000 0.007 % (0.027 %). Turned down: 5 or 7 radii from 5 % to 95 % (gap 0.082 and 0.071 %; with 7 a Morgenstern-Price value 10.7 % below Bishop on V036d), today's family with 11 radii (no gain), and a first pass of 500 or 750 iterations (at 500 V104a Bishop missed its basin by 7.4 %, also with more refinement). Decided by the maintainer.

### 2026-11-12: A side of the search given as a point has one point; a point on a corner counts as a point (issue #157)

A search side whose limits are one x has that one point in the first pass, instead of all the grid's points of that side coinciding there; the other side and the radii stay as for a range, and the budget of the coinciding points is not spent elsewhere (option C). A point on a corner of the ground, which `Slope::search_limits` widens by 1e-5 of the model's width, is still a point: its circles go through the two ends of the widened limit (in effect the corner, on either side of it), not through 16 points spread over those 0.3 mm, and the refinement and the local search do not step on that side. Option C alone changes no result (duplicates were removed before evaluation); the corner rule moves the models with a point limit on a corner, in the verification set only V054 (−2e-6 %, 486 surfaces instead of 1 691). On 25 imported models with a point limit the mean distance above the best F known goes from 0.114 to 0.099 % with 8 % less run time and 43 % fewer surfaces. Turned down: giving the freed budget to the other side (A: 0.102 %) or to the other side and more radii (B: 0.093 %), which cost 70–80 % more surfaces for mixed results, since the refinement's step follows the point spacing. Decided by the maintainer.

### 2026-11-11: A single exit x on a vertical ground face exits at the foot of the face (issue #141)

When the exit range is a single x (exitLeftX = exitRightX) on a vertical segment of the ground surface that descends in the sliding direction (in canonical, left-to-right space: the ground just left of x above the ground just right of it) and is not one of the model's own end faces, the exit point of the search is the foot of the face moved up the face by 1e-5 of the model's width (at most half the face's height), `Slope::exit_face_point`. A circle through that point ends there (`select_entry_exit` ends a circle where it comes out of the soil); one exactly through the foot would run on in the soil beyond it. It is the vertical analogue of the corner rule of `search_limits`, which keeps [x, x] for such an exit instead of moving its left end by 1e-5 · width (that put the exit on the crest). The grid search, the refinement, the local search and the exit vertex of the non-circular optimisation take the exit's ground point from `Slope::exit_ground_point`; block surfaces and the optimisation's layer seeds end where their passive segment meets the ground and are checked against the x-limits only, as before. The desktop draws the exit marker at the foot (the sliding direction there taken from the entry range's side of the exit). Before, the exit was at mid-height of the face (the ground's y at a vertical step is the mean of its y values) or on its crest, so a wall's internal stability search gave far too high factors of safety or no surface. Files of other programs give a wall's exit as the point at the foot of its face; the importers keep only its x, and need no change. The rule is unconditional, with no file-format change; models without such an exit, faces that rise in the sliding direction and exit ranges are unchanged (no verification result moved). Turned down or deferred: optional y values in the limits (a file-format change) for an exit range along a face or an entry on a face; ranges measured by arc length along the ground (it would move the results of every model whose range spans a vertical step); spending the first-pass search budget differently for a single exit point (it moves results); any change to which thin wedges next to a wall are accepted. Decided by the maintainer.

### 2026-11-11: The tension-only rule of reinforcement is for passive elements; active elements always carry their force (issue #143)

Where the movement along a slip surface would push an element (it lies past the normal to the slice base on the side the mass moves towards), a passive element (`application: "passive"`, divided by F) carries nothing beyond 2° past the normal and acts along itself within 2°, as before. An active element (`application` "active" or absent, not divided by F: a prestressed anchor) carries its full force on every surface it crosses; on the pushed side the force acts along the element whatever its direction setting, elsewhere the setting applies, and where part of the force then acts in the direction of sliding it lowers F. Sources: a prestressed anchor is a known external force with no angle criterion (FHWA GEC 4, Sabatini et al. 1999, §5.7.3.1, p. 97; Duncan, Wright & Brandon 2014, ch. 8, §8.1–8.2; Wright & Duncan 1991, TRR 1330), and Hoek's plane failure with a tensioned anchor (*Practical Rock Engineering*) takes it at its real angle to the normal with no restriction; compression in a nail is not counted (Recommendations Clouterre 1991, §3.2.2.3, pp. 124–126; FHWA GEC 7, Lazarte et al. 2015, §6.3.3c, p. 149). The 2° tolerance is cSlope's own and stricter than Clouterre's 10–20° for nails (§2.2.3, §3.2.2.3); the passive threshold, geosynthetics and the active elements' direction on the pushed side wait for decisions of their own (issue #143). Turned down: dropping the rule for all elements (a passive element in compression would count as stabilising, which Clouterre excludes); keeping it for active anchors (no source, and F jumped between neighbouring circles, from 1.233 to 0.956 on an anchored wall). Implemented in `apply_reinforcement` (model/reinforcement.rs).

### 2026-11-11: The steep ends of a circle are not cut into narrower slices

The slices of a circle are the plain ones: the nominal slice division plus the breaks at the geometry, the material boundaries and the water lines, with no extra cuts where the arc turns steep (the cut of any arc interval wider than max(2°, 2b/R), from 2026-11-02, is removed). This supersedes the "steep ends still cut" part of the 2026-11-10 decision below and brings cSlope in line with other programs. On 44 public .gsz analyses, on the reference program's own critical circles, the mean deviation goes from −0.040 % to +0.005 %, and N′ is negative on the critical surface of 11 analyses instead of 25. Against the verification checks' program references the mean absolute deviation goes from 0.319 to 0.313 % and the RMS from 0.461 to 0.455 % (144 results moved, 106 of them closer); against the literature the mean absolute deviation goes from 0.474 to 0.494 % and the RMS from 0.699 to 0.725 %. One check leaves its tolerance: V068, Bishop on Low's (1989) published circle, 1.1423 → 1.1607 (+1.81 % against Low's 1.14, tolerance 1.44 %). Its centre lies at crest level, so the upper end is a vertical scarp where the factor depends on how the steep part is sliced (Low calls the Bishop value there misleading). Accepted by a documented tolerance rule rather than a finer slicing for that surface: a problem whose reference surface has a vertical or near-vertical end is marked `slicingSensitive` and gets + 1 % (packages/engine/src/verification/tolerance.ts), set on V068 only, whose tolerance becomes 2.44 %. Two unit tests whose premise was a slice at a vertical end now run with more slices at the same tolerance (the m_α rejection in round3-engine.test.ts, 160 slices; the submerged vertical cut against the buoyant cut in verification.test.ts, 200 slices). The slice-count convergence test on the V068 geometry (50 slices within 1 % of 500) is removed with the cuts it tested: at a vertical end 50 slices now sit 1.9 % above 500. Implemented in packages/engine-rs/crates/cslope-engine/src/model/slices.rs; recorded in VERIFICATION-LOG.md.

### 2026-11-11: Rapid drawdown: the Kc = 1 envelope from the R envelope by Duncan, Wright & Brandon (2014), Eqs. 9.6–9.7

In the three-stage rapid drawdown the undrained strength at isotropic consolidation is the R envelope (cR, φR) expressed against the effective normal stress on the failure plane at consolidation, σ′fc: τff = d + σ′fc·tan ψ with d = cR·cos φR·cos φ′ / (1 − sin φR) and tan ψ = sin φR·cos φ′ / (1 − sin φR) (Duncan, Wright & Brandon 2014, ch. 9, Eqs. 9.6–9.7). The R envelope is drawn tangent to the total-stress Mohr circles and the strength on the failure plane is τff = ½(σ1f − σ3f)·cos φ′ (Eq. 9.5), which gives the factor cos φ′. The engine used cos φR in its place, which made the Kc = 1 strength too high by cos φR / cos φ′ (for φ′ = 45°, φR = 23° by 30 %). Checked against Pilarcitos Dam (Duncan, Wright & Wong 1990: c′ = 0, φ′ = 45°, cR = 60 psf, φR = 23° give d = 64 psf, ψ = 24.4°; computed 64.10 psf and 24.39°), now a unit test. On the reference critical circle of the Walter Bouldin Dam example F falls from 1.0457 to 1.0226 (reference value 1.0260, published 1.04), and the per-slice undrained strengths come within 1 % of the reference's (were 2–3 % above). Only models with a drawdown (R) envelope move; no verification problem has one. Approved by the maintainer.

### 2026-11-11: The unit weight of water is a per-model analysis option

γw is `AnalysisOptions.waterUnitWeight` (kN/m³, finite and > 0, else an error; absent: `WATER_UNIT_WEIGHT`, 9.807), not a `SlopeDefinition` field, because the comparable model-level physical inputs already live there: the seismic coefficients kh and kv, the pore pressure head convention and the water in a tension crack are analysis options, and the app edits them in one panel (Model › Analysis › Options, *Water and tension crack*), where the new field sits. The slope takes it from the options and gives it to everything that turns a head into a pressure: the slices (pore pressure, suction, free water, water in a crack, the Rankine crack depth), the waternet, the effective stress of reinforcement and SHANSEP, the earlier stage and the state before rapid drawdown (the same water), and the finite element models and bounds. `validate` warns outside 9–11 kN/m³ (a unit mistake, mostly). Projects store it only when a model sets its own value, so files and input hashes of models at the default do not change, and nothing moves for a model without it (the verification is unchanged). The importers take the file's γw (in kN/m³) into the model instead of noting the difference, and the exporters write the model's. Turned down: a `SlopeDefinition` field (the definition holds geometry, materials, water lines and loads; the physical constants that are settings sit with the options, and the earlier stage would carry a second copy that could disagree); a global app preference (a project would give other results on another machine).

### 2026-11-11: The unit weight of water is 9.807 kN/m³

γw = ρw·g with ρw = 1000 kg/m³ and standard gravity g = 9.80665 m/s² (CGPM 1901), rounded to 9.807 kN/m³, in the engine and everywhere the app writes or compares it (the importers' unit checks and notes, the exporters, the docs and the verification report). Was 9.81. Problems that publish their own unit weights keep them; the engine has no per-model γw. Turned down: keeping 9.81 (0.03 % more pore pressure than standard gravity gives).

### 2026-11-10: The slice base of a circle is the straight chord, with the shear arm to the chord

A slice of a circle stands on the straight chord between its two base points, the conventional discretisation of a circle and the only behaviour: the weight is that of the soil above the chord, the base length the chord's length, α the chord's inclination, and the pore pressure and strength are taken at the chord's midpoint. The base shear force's moment arm about the centre is the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord, R·cos(Δθ/2) (Δθ the angle the slice subtends), and the normal force's arm is 0, after the general limit equilibrium formulation of Fredlund & Krahn (1977), where the shear force's arm is measured to the base line; every moment equation (ordinary, Bishop, Spencer, Morgenstern-Price) takes the per-slice arm. The weight's arm stays W·(x − xc). The steep ends of a circle are still cut into narrower slices, so that a chord does not cut far below a steeply turning arc (the published V068 and the accuracy checks of a submerged vertical cut need it). (No longer: see 2026-11-11, the steep ends are not cut.) On the same circle F rises by about 0.01–0.15 % (mean +0.014 % over the golden circle analyses); on 45 example surfaces the gap to the conventional method's values on the same surfaces falls from −0.10 % to −0.05 % on average (median −0.02 %). The arc-consistent base (arc length, α at the middle of the arc, the weight under the arc, arm R; from 2026-11-02) and the experiment switch `CSLOPE_SLICE_BASE` are removed. Turned down: the chord without the end cut (gap −0.004 %, but V068 1.8 % from Low (1989) at 50 slices and a coarser discretisation at steep ends); keeping the arc (it converges faster with the slice count, but its results differ from the conventional method's on the same slices, which users compare with); making the base an option (a setting that moves results for little gain, and a second code path to test and document). Recorded in VERIFICATION-LOG.md.

### 2026-11-07: The browser page's `.cslp` writer is a second WebAssembly module, loaded on demand (issue #107)

"Download .cslp" in the browser writes the project with the Rust writer of `cslope-project` (feature `write`: the ZIP writer and miniz_oxide's deflate in memory, no files), so the page writes what the app and the Python package write. In the engine's module it added 2.4 MB (5.8 MB, above the 4 MB budget of ARCHITECTURE.md §3.5): the app's model with its strict reading and the writer are mostly serde code. It is a module of its own, `crates/cslope-project-wasm` (2.1 MB, 0.4 MB brotli, built for size), committed in `packages/engine/src/wasm-project` and fetched by the page only when a project is downloaded. The page's result is saved with the input hash of the definition it ran (a level water table written as the line it stands for, missing colours as the model's), and the save policy keeps it only when the model reads back as that input: the app opens it current, and its own run gives the same F (`web-project.test.ts`). Turned down: the writer in the engine (over budget); a writer in TypeScript in the page (a second writer of the format to keep equal to the app's); saving a stale result marked out of date (the Rust writer's save policy drops one, and a result the page no longer shows is of no use in the app).

### 2026-11-06: D-23, the public defaults are the app's (issue #55)

`cslope.analyse` in Python and the command line take the app's options for a new model (`APP_DEFAULT_ANALYSIS_OPTIONS` in packages/desktop/src/store/defaults.ts: Morgenstern-Price, 30 slices, 1000 + 500 trial surfaces, composite surfaces) as the defaults, under the options of an analysis document and those given; `public_default_options()` in the engine (types/analysis.rs) holds them, and `cslope.default_options()` returns them. The same model then gives the same F in the app and in Python. The engine's internal default (`default_analysis_options`: Bishop, 25 slices, circles) is unchanged, so the verification problems, the golden results and `cslope.Slope` keep it. Turned down: changing the engine's default (it would move every result that relies on it, and the verification options); keeping the engine's defaults in Python (an agent's run would differ from the app's without a word). Noted as a change in the Python README.

### 2026-11-06: D-24, `freeWater` replaces `followBoundary`; a boundary's `materialName` is deprecated (issue #55)

`waterTable.followBoundary` (and that of `waterTables` entries) reads the wrong way round: true means *no* free water. A `freeWater` field with the documented meaning is added (true: water above the ground is free water whose weight acts on the ground; false, the default: the line is clipped to the ground); it takes precedence, and `followBoundary` is read only without it. A `waterTables` entry with neither takes the main line's `freeWater` when that is given (the app writes `followBoundary` on every line and never `freeWater`, so its files read as before); the app reads `freeWater` when it opens a definition (store/definition-model.ts). `materialBoundaries[].materialName` is optional (absent is "") and deprecated: with a name and no `regionAssignments` the engine assigns the region below the boundary's midpoint. The engine reads both old fields as before, the schema marks them deprecated, the examples show only the new names, and `validate` reports `followBoundary: true` (a warning) and a `materialName` that the engine would use (an error). No app change: the app still writes `followBoundary` and `materialName: ""`. Turned down: removing the old names (older files and the app's own output use them); renaming without the engine reading both (a breaking change for the app).

### 2026-11: A slip surface may not leave through the model's side

With "Both" directions and no search limits, a surface could exit through a vertical side of the model. `VERTICAL_SIDE_SLOPE` in `geometry_ops.rs` rejects it; the verification results did not move.
