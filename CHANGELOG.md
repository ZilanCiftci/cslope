# Changelog

All notable changes to cSlope. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org).

## [0.1.0-beta.1] - 2026-10-16

Beta of 0.1.0, to test the program and the signed Windows installer before the first public release. It has the contents listed under 0.1.0 in CHANGELOG.md.

## [0.1.0] - Unreleased

The first public release.

### Analysis

- Bishop simplified, Janbu simplified with the f₀ correction, and Morgenstern–Price with constant (Spencer), half-sine, clipped-sine, trapezoidal and user-defined interslice functions.
- Circular and non-circular slip surfaces: search between entry and exit ranges, custom circles and polylines, non-circular optimisation, minimum slip depth and minimum sliding area, analysis in both slip directions.
- Circular search: for each entry and exit pair also the circles tangent to every material interface (the top of a stronger, high-strength or impenetrable layer), and the refinement and local search start from the best circle of each basin besides the best overall, so a toe circle above a stronger layer is not lost to a deep circle of nearly the same FOS. The number of surfaces left out by the minimum slip area or depth is shown in the results and the PDF summary.
- Search and optimisation: the entry points behind loads come on top of the search grid instead of replacing grid points (each load used to coarsen the grid, also loads outside the search limits). Surfaces that leave the model through its base are rejected: polylines and circles are checked against every corner and edge of the boundary below the ground (a segment or arc could pass below a re-entrant corner, or a little below a sloping base, between the points checked), and a surface with a slice base below the ground but outside the model gets no slices instead of the strength of a fallback material. The non-circular optimisation runs to its minimum: the best surface is optimised further while a cycle still lowers its FOS by more than the tolerance, refined once more to 33 vertices and then restarted from its result (lowers V050b 1.228 → 1.225, V051 0.698 → 0.695, V094 1.402 → 1.400; 21 non-circular results 0.1–0.8 % lower, about 10 % more run time). These are deeper minima of the same models, so they sit further below the other programs' values on average: the mean absolute error against them rises from 0.398 to 0.408 % (VERIFICATION-LOG.md). The engine's default search refines 500 surfaces around the best (refinedIterations), as the desktop app does; with the old default of 0, API users got a factor of safety 0.5–4 % too high.
- Morgenstern–Price and Spencer return the factor where moment and force equilibrium meet, interpolated between the last two λ, instead of the moment factor at the first λ within the tolerance; the default tolerance is 0.001 (was 0.005). On fixed non-circular surfaces the factor was up to 0.7 % off the exact solution, now within 0.02 %.
- Ito & Matsui pile rows: the force acts at the resultant of the soil pressure on the pile above the slip surface, not at the slip surface, where its arm was the longest possible (about 5 % too much pile moment on Cai & Ugai's slope); the earlier placement remains as an option ("Force acts at").
- Block search: non-circular surfaces through search objects (fixed points, lines and windows), with end segments to the ground at the Rankine angles 45° ± φ/2 or within given ranges, refined around the best and optionally optimised as free polylines; for failures along weak layers (Options › Search method).
- Tension cracks with a fixed or automatic (Rankine) depth, dry or water-filled.
- Partial factors on strength and loads, with presets for Swedish practice (EKS/IEG) and EN 1997-1 DA3.
- Sensitivity analysis, and probabilistic analysis with the Taylor series and Monte Carlo methods.
- Ito & Matsui pile rows take the effective overburden (γ′ below the water table) instead of the total; a Morgenstern–Price surface where moment and force equilibrium never meet keeps its closest-pair factor but is reported as not converged, with the gap in the results panel and the PDF summary.
- Bishop on a non-circular critical surface (a custom polyline or non-circular optimisation) gives a result warning, and the Options window a hint, that its factor of safety depends on the moment axis; Spencer or Morgenstern–Price is recommended there.
- Lime-cement columns with partial factors: each part of the strength takes its own factor (γcu on the undrained strength of soil and columns, γc′ on the columns' c′, γφ′ on tan φ′) instead of γcu on the whole cohesion, in the slices, the Rankine crack depth and the Ito & Matsui pile force; the pile force of a combined material (lime-cement columns in their drained limit, Mohr–Coulomb with a c-factor) is the lower of the drained and undrained pressure, as on the slice bases.
- Slices: the kinks and vertical steps of every water line above the slip surface are slice edges, so the pore pressure, the saturated weight and ponded water are exact per slice and a step (a wall, a sheet pile) gets no cos²θ correction; a slice base exactly on a layer boundary takes the weaker of the two soils (never a high-strength or impenetrable layer below); fixed slices are also split at layer and water crossings; long stretches are still cut to about the nominal slice width when a densely digitised water line uses up the slice count.

### Model

- Exterior and interior boundaries with snapping, DXF import, and parameters with expressions.
- Material models: Mohr–Coulomb, undrained, strength increasing with depth (from the ground or from the top of the layer) or from a datum, with a strength cutoff, anisotropic, spatially varying, lime-cement columns, and high-strength and impenetrable (bedrock) layers. Saturated unit weight below the water table.
- Water: several piezometric lines with one per material if needed, ponded water above the ground.
- Ponded water above the ground comes from the line of the material at the ground, the line that also gives that material its pore pressure, in the analysis (weight on the ground, horizontal thrust, tension-crack depth) and in the drawings; a material's own line above the ground used to give the base the pore pressure of the pond without its weight.
- Distributed and line loads; anchors, grouted tiebacks, soil nails, geosynthetics, pile rows and sheet pile walls.
- Edit view: coordinate labels find a free place outside the fill, clear of other labels, the boundary lines, entry/exit dots, markers, loads, elements, water lines, water symbols and custom surfaces, and points off the screen get none; vertex dots are drawn over the search-limit markers; interior boundaries are drawn only inside the model; a custom circle shows dashed only from where it enters the ground to where it leaves it, on a halo that keeps it visible on dark layers; a fixed tension-crack depth shows as a labelled dimension on the crest instead of a dashed line along the whole ground; with only custom surfaces analysed the search limits are hidden; long ruler labels are set smaller, and left out only when they still do not fit, so no label is cut; load handles are picked over the arrow height as drawn.

### Results and reporting

- The paper takes in the critical circle's centre and FOS label when they are shown, widening a fitted frame after a run if needed.
- Critical surface, all surfaces or a factor-of-safety filter, slices, annotations and templates.
- Plots of slice data and of Fm and Ff against λ; export to PDF and PNG, slice data to Excel and CSV.
- Result warnings, in the results panel and the PDF summary: a material boundary that ends inside a region (it does not split it), and slices of the critical surface with negative effective normal force (N′ < 0) or m_α below 0.2. Boundary ends within 1e-3 of the model size of the outline or another boundary are put on it, so a layer is no longer lost to a small gap.
- The PDF report ends with an analysis summary: method, search, water and tension crack, partial factors, materials and loads. With partial factors, the PDF and the slice data mark design values.
- The paper frames the model at true scale with a margin, including water lines, loads and reinforcement, and spaces its axis ticks for their labels; PNG export is 2400 px wide on white. "Use model bounds" and the refit after the geometry grows give the same frame the model starts with, below the title block. Fit in the edit view ignores water lines far below the model, and the parts of a water line below its base, and keeps clear of the toolbar.
- Result plot: the sliding mass follows stepped and vertical faces, a tension crack is drawn as part of the critical surface with the water in it, slice lines show on dark layers, and the search limits point into their ranges under the result.
- Loads and reinforcement forces on the canvas and in the PDF: touching load strips draw as one load with one label ("q = 20–40 kPa"), box heights follow the magnitude and magnitudes are rounded; force arrows sit beside the element they act along, on one side and within 30 % of the spacing in a dense wall so each reads as its own element's, clear of the slip line, the element itself, material boundaries, other elements and each other and never longer than the element; each force value is written at its element's head, outside the slope on the element's line, or beside its arrow when the slip surface crosses the element far from the head, and where elements are closer than the text height the values form a column with a leader to each element number, so no value is left out ("÷ FOS", drawn so it cannot read as "+", marks a force divided by the factor of safety; forces of 0.0 kN/m are left out); force labels keep clear of the search-limit markers and a margin inside the frame; element numbers, on a light halo, keep clear of a vertex dot at the head and move off lines and markers.
- Water-table symbols, one or two per line on its flat parts, prefer open water, then the soil outside the sliding mass, keep clear of the critical surface, vertex dots, prescribed surfaces, search markers, loads, reinforcement, thin layers and the model's sides, and leave out the ≡ where the water is too shallow for it. Over a result the search-limit triangles stand above the ground, a limit shared by both ranges is drawn once and no dots lie on the sliding mass. A tension crack is drawn only where the analysis cut the surface at one, with its water in the crack; a thin sliding mass is widened only under the ground, coincident or dense slice lines are thinned, and outlines have no mitre spikes. The PDF draws loads, search markers and water symbols as the canvas does.
- Ponded water shows small load arrows pointing at the ground, normal to it and scaled with the depth, clear of the water-table symbols, on the canvas and in the PDF.
- Drawing details: in the edit view a search-limit triangle at a vertex moves off the vertex dot; with a fixed tension crack a custom surface starts at the crack's base, as the analysis takes it, and the crack dimension stands at that crack (otherwise on the crest with its bottom clear of layer boundaries), labelled next to it; slice boundaries closer than 2.5 px (0.7 mm in the PDF) are drawn as one line; the band of a thin sliding mass ends at the slip line's ends.

### Interface

- A failed run shows its error over the edit canvas and in the status bar, with a shortcut to the search limits when no slip surface was found. F5 or Ctrl+Enter runs the analysis; Escape closes the dialog windows and Model Properties. A new project starts with a neutral "Model 1" instead of an example's name and title.

### Verification

- 177 verification problems with 356 reference values from the literature and from other programs, run by the automated tests on every change.
- Inputs corrected against the program manuals' figures: the water table of the Sharma (1996) slope (V082a–c, 1.1–1.7 ft lower), the lacustrine clay of the James Bay dyke 6.5 m thick (V039, V040), phreatic correction on ACADS 4 (V051) and the circle radius of the USACE dam (V087, 169.0 ft). Program values computed on the earlier inputs are no longer checks. A further input audit of all 164 problems against their sources corrected V005, V009a–c, V017a/b, V042, V061, V065, V068, V075, V077, V083 and V092, and the reference or check of V029, V060a, V066c and V098. More published values are checks: Morgenstern–Price (half-sine) on V082b, V082c, V086b, V086c, V088–V090, V092, V096a and V096b, Janbu simplified on V085 and V093, and a new problem V082d with the program's circular search between exit limits; V095b, a duplicate of V095a, is merged into it; V016c's block-search values, which belong to a search type cSlope does not have, are no longer checks.
- A published value that is confirmed in its source but contradicts the source's own rigorous results for the same surface is shown as disputed, with the reason, and left out of the error statistics (Zhu & Lee's simplified Bishop value 1.429 for surface 3).
