# Changelog

All notable changes to cSlope. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org).

## [Unreleased]

- The steep ends of a circle are no longer cut into narrower slices: the slices of a circle are the nominal division plus the breaks at layers, water lines and the ground, as in other programs. Factors of safety of circular surfaces move by typically less than 0.2 % (mostly up); on a circle that ends in a vertical scarp the factor depends more on the slice count (the verification problem V068 on Low's published circle: 1.142 → 1.161 with 50 slices). Non-circular surfaces do not change.
- The unit weight of water is a setting of each model: **Unit weight of water (kN/m³)** under Water and tension crack in Model › Analysis › Options (default 9.807; for example 9.82 for local gravity or 10 as a simplification). It sets the pore pressure, free water, water in a tension crack, head lines, reinforcement and the finite element analyses, and the analysis summary and PDF show it. Projects store it only when a model sets its own; older projects open unchanged. Imports from .gsz, .sli and .stix take the file's value instead of noting it, and the exports write the model's. Python: `Options.water_unit_weight`; analysis documents: `options.waterUnitWeight`.
- The unit weight of water is 9.807 kN/m³ (ρw·g with standard gravity), was 9.81; results with water move by a few hundredths of a percent. Exported files carry 9.807.
- The slices of a circle stand on the straight chord between their base points, with the base shear's moment arm the distance from the centre to the chord (Fredlund & Krahn 1977), as the conventional method has it; the arc-consistent base is gone (the steep ends of a circle are still cut into narrower slices). Factors of safety of circular surfaces rise slightly, typically 0.01–0.15 %; non-circular surfaces do not change.
- .gsz import takes an analysis's phreatic correction (IsPiezPhreatic) as the pore pressure head *phreatic correction*, and the .gsz export writes it for a model that uses it.

### Fixed

- .gsz import: an analysis whose slip surfaces are taken from another analysis (slip surface option FromOther) uses that analysis's search (entry and exit ranges, fully specified surfaces or block search, with its direction and number of slices), following a chain of such analyses; before, cSlope searched the whole slope and could find a much smaller surface. When the other analysis is not in the file, or the chain loops, the import message says so and the whole slope is searched as before.
- Rapid drawdown (three-stage): the strength at isotropic consolidation (Kc = 1) is taken from the R envelope as in Duncan, Wright & Brandon (2014), Eqs. 9.6–9.7, with the factor cos φ′ on both its intercept and its slope; it had cos φR in its place and was too high by cos φR / cos φ′ (30 % for φ′ = 45°, φR = 23°). Factors of safety of models with a drawdown (R) envelope fall: on the Walter Bouldin Dam example from 1.046 to 1.023 on the reference circle (reference value 1.026). Models without an R envelope do not change.

## [0.1.0-beta.2] - 2026-11-04

Second beta of 0.1.0. It has the contents listed under 0.1.0 below; since beta 1:

- The analysis engine is written in Rust and runs as WebAssembly: the same results (all verification checks as before), 3 to 5 times faster on one thread.
- One analysis runs on several cores (up to 8), with the same result as on one; Run all analyzes several models at a time, longest first, on workers kept between runs (20 models on 4 cores: 2.4 s, before the engine change 14 s).

- Finite element analysis (experimental, marked so wherever it appears): limit analysis bounds, strength reduction, seepage, reinforcement, staged construction.
- Import and export of .gsz, .stix and .sli/.slim/.slmd project files.
- Number fields accept a decimal comma in any language; the slice table's shear force comes from the engine.
- Security: ZIP and file size limits on import, a stricter content security policy, project files keep only known fields, Electron 45 with the app archive checked against the signed program at every start.
- Verification: 187 problems; V056's coarse-mesh finite element reference is shown as disputed and not counted.

### Analysis

- Bishop simplified, Janbu simplified with the f₀ correction, and Morgenstern–Price with constant (Spencer), half-sine, clipped-sine, trapezoidal and user-defined interslice functions.
- Circular and non-circular slip surfaces: search between entry and exit ranges, custom circles and polylines, non-circular optimization, minimum slip depth and minimum slip area, analysis in both slip directions.
- Circular search: for each entry and exit pair also the circles tangent to every material interface (the top of a stronger, high-strength or impenetrable layer), and the refinement and local search start from the best circle of each basin besides the best overall, so a toe circle above a stronger layer is not lost to a deep circle of nearly the same FOS. The number of surfaces left out by the minimum slip area or depth is shown in the results and the PDF summary.
- The minimum slip area (the smallest sliding mass analyzed, cross-section area per meter) defaults to 0, no limit, instead of the automatic 1 % of the squared ground relief: that filter had no basis in the literature and could hide a real local failure, such as a low cut at the toe of a high slope. A limit can still be set. Projects saved with the automatic setting (an empty field) now analyze the small surfaces too, and their results can move. In the verification 10 of 414 checks move, all of them critical surfaces that are thin slides parallel to a cohesionless face and now come closer to the infinite-slope factor: V005 2.519 → 2.506 (theory 2.50), V032b 1.156 → 1.155 (1.155 and 1.15), V052a 1.960 → 1.948 (1.948–1.951; infinite slope 1.9475), V034a by 5e-7; mean absolute error against the other programs 0.334 % → 0.327 %, against the papers 0.490 % → 0.476 %.
- Search and optimization: the entry points behind loads come on top of the search grid instead of replacing grid points (each load used to coarsen the grid, also loads outside the search limits). Surfaces that leave the model through its base are rejected: polylines and circles are checked against every corner and edge of the boundary below the ground (a segment or arc could pass below a re-entrant corner, or a little below a sloping base, between the points checked), and a surface with a slice base below the ground but outside the model gets no slices instead of the strength of a fallback material. The non-circular optimization runs to its minimum: the best surface is optimized further while a cycle still lowers its FOS by more than the tolerance, refined once more to 33 vertices and then restarted from its result (lowers V050b 1.228 → 1.225, V051 0.698 → 0.695, V094 1.402 → 1.400; 21 non-circular results 0.1–0.8 % lower, about 10 % more run time). These are deeper minima of the same models, so they sit further below the other programs' values on average: the mean absolute error against them rises from 0.398 to 0.408 % (VERIFICATION-LOG.md). The engine's default search refines 500 surfaces around the best (refinedIterations), as the desktop app does; with the old default of 0, API users got a factor of safety 0.5–4 % too high.
- Morgenstern–Price and Spencer return the factor where moment and force equilibrium meet, interpolated between the last two λ, instead of the moment factor at the first λ within the tolerance; the default tolerance is 0.001 (was 0.005). On fixed non-circular surfaces the factor was up to 0.7 % off the exact solution, now within 0.02 %.
- Morgenstern–Price and Spencer accept a solution only where the moment and the force factor are both positive and differ by less than the tolerance, capped at 0.01 and at 1 % of the factor (Morgenstern & Price 1965 and Fredlund & Krahn 1977 define the solution by Fm = Ff; the tolerance is its precision). A closest pair also needs both factors positive. The tolerance used to apply to the gap as given: at a loose tolerance (0.2) a surface was accepted with Fm = −0.003 and Ff = 0.19, and a change of one slice in the last bit moved its factor anywhere between −4.2 and 13.9; such a surface now has no solution, and at tolerances up to 0.1 the factor stays within 1 % of the exact crossing. At the default tolerance 0.001 the cap binds only for F < 0.1: no verification result moved (VERIFICATION-LOG.md).
- Ito & Matsui pile rows: the force acts at the resultant of the soil pressure on the pile above the slip surface, not at the slip surface, where its arm was the longest possible (about 5 % too much pile moment on Cai & Ugai's slope); the earlier placement remains as an option ("Force acts at").
- Block search: non-circular surfaces through search objects (fixed points, lines and windows), with end segments to the ground at the Rankine angles 45° ± φ/2 or within given ranges, refined around the best and optionally optimized as free polylines; for failures along weak layers (Options › Search method).
- Finite elements (Options › Finite elements), experimental: still under development, its results not verified for design and marked so in the options, the result panel and on the canvas. They give the factor of safety from the lower and upper bounds of limit analysis (associated flow, second-order cone programs solved by Clarabel), refined where the gap between them lies; they converge as the mesh is refined. The same bounds with Davis B's reduced strength stand for non-associated flow, usually conservatively. Strength reduction with the dilation angle (non-associated flow, the method of other programs) is shown for comparison on the first mesh only: its factor falls on every mesh refinement without a limit (Sysala et al. 2021; on Pruska's slope 58-5 0.692, 0.634 and 0.603 at element sizes H/15, H/30 and H/45, while the associated bounds converge to 0.73–0.74). New projects have the bounds on and Davis B flow, the option marked recommended; earlier projects keep their settings, and a project file without a flow rule reads as non-associated, as before.
- Lower bound of limit analysis: the ends of strip loads are singular points of the ground surface like its corners, and every singular point gets a fan of elements (those within an element size bisected until each spans at most 30° as seen from it). A strip load ending a few millimeters or centimeters from a crest corner, or anywhere on a straight surface where only two or three elements met at its end, found no admissible stress field at any F: the jump in the load had to be carried by the discontinuities between those elements alone. Near corners the bound was also too low and depended on the mesh: on an embankment's crest with the load at its corner, 2 mm and 5 cm from it the bound is now 1.13, 1.13 and 1.10 (was 0.91, 0.87 and none; upper bound 1.21), and on a weightless 45° wedge loaded to Prandtl's c′N_c (F = 1) 0.93 (was 0.89), and under Prandtl's strip footing 0.94 (was 0.77).
- Tension cracks with a fixed or automatic (Rankine) depth, dry or water-filled.
- Partial factors on strength and loads, with presets for Swedish practice (EKS/IEG) and EN 1997-1 DA3.
- Partial factors in Swedish practice from the safety class and the conversion factors η: γcu = 1.5/η, γc′ = γφ′ = 1.3/η and γQ = γQ/γd · γd with γd = 0.83, 0.91 and 1.0 in safety class 1, 2 and 3 (γQ/γd 1.3, 1.4 or 1.5). The inputs are saved with the factors and shown in the summary; entering a factor by hand leaves them.
- 3D effects of temporary slopes after SGI Vägledning 8 (Temporära slänter, 2024): F3D over the circular surfaces of the analysis for a stage of length L between two plane end faces of cohesive soil, F_P = F_2D + 2 ∫c_u r dA / (L M_E) and F_3D = F_2D + k·0.75 (F_P − F_2D), with infinitely (k = 1, eq. 2-3) or finitely overstrong adjacent stability (eq. 2-4), and materials (a dry crust) that can be left out of the end faces. The smallest F3D and its surface are shown with the result and in the summary.
- Shallow slides in cohesionless soil: a warning when the critical surface is a thin slide, its base on average less than 1 m (or a tenth of the ground relief around it) below the ground with half its base or more in cohesionless soil (c′ = 0, not the undrained strength of a combined analysis), as the search finds in a cohesionless road bank's edge before the deeper mechanism; it points to the minimum slip depth, slip area and the minimum entry–exit distance, which is now in the options (it was in the engine and the summary only).
- Sensitivity analysis, and probabilistic analysis with the Taylor series and Monte Carlo methods.
- Ito & Matsui pile rows take the effective overburden (γ′ below the water table) instead of the total; a Morgenstern–Price surface where moment and force equilibrium never meet keeps its closest-pair factor but is reported as not converged, with the gap in the results panel and the PDF summary.
- Bishop on a non-circular critical surface (a custom polyline or non-circular optimization) gives a result warning, and the Options window a hint, that its factor of safety depends on the moment axis; Spencer or Morgenstern–Price is recommended there.
- Lime-cement columns with partial factors: each part of the strength takes its own factor (γcu on the undrained strength of soil and columns, γc′ on the columns' c′, γφ′ on tan φ′) instead of γcu on the whole cohesion, in the slices, the Rankine crack depth and the Ito & Matsui pile force; the pile force of a combined material (lime-cement columns in their drained limit, Mohr–Coulomb with a c-factor) is the lower of the drained and undrained pressure, as on the slice bases.
- Slices: the kinks and vertical steps of every water line above the slip surface are slice edges, so the pore pressure, the saturated weight and ponded water are exact per slice and a step (a wall, a sheet pile) gets no cos²θ correction; a slice base exactly on a layer boundary takes the weaker of the two soils (never a high-strength or impenetrable layer below); fixed slices are also split at layer and water crossings; long stretches are still cut to about the nominal slice width when a densely digitised water line uses up the slice count.
- The engine computes the same bits on every platform: its trigonometric, exponential and power functions come from the pure-Rust libm crate, the same as in WebAssembly, instead of the platform's C library. The Python package on Windows and macOS gave the Talbingo Dam 1.95079 against the app's 1.95074; it now gives the app's value. No verification result moved.
- The analysis engine is written in Rust and runs in the app as WebAssembly, replacing the TypeScript engine: the same methods and results (all 414 verification checks and the finite element references as before, the published table unchanged), about 3 to 5 times faster on one thread (a design-review embankment: 1.70 s before, 0.38 s now; with 7000 trial surfaces 8.1 s before, 1.7 s now). The app no longer carries the separate HiGHS and Clarabel solvers: the limit analysis bounds are solved by Clarabel inside the engine (an older project that asked for HiGHS gets Clarabel's bounds, about 1 % apart).

### Model

- Exterior and interior boundaries with snapping, DXF import, and parameters with expressions.
- Drawings (underlays): Model › Drawing places a section drawing (PNG, JPEG, TIFF, or a page of a PDF) under the model, to trace the model over it. The Place drawing window lists the project's drawings and adds files, shows a PDF's pages, crops the section, and calibrates it by its axes (x and y scaled separately, for sections drawn with vertical exaggeration; a second section on the same sheet can reuse the scale), by two or more points (least-squares fit with the misfit of each point), or by an origin and a scale; with a magnifier, a crosshair and a preview over the model's outline. The drawing is drawn above the soil colors and below the model's lines at its opacity, locked so clicks go to the model; the Drawings section of the Models panel shows, hides, locks, renames, reorders, recalibrates and removes them, and can show one in the result view and the PDF export. One file is stored once in the project file however many models place it (each with its own page, crop and calibration); a project file with drawings may be up to 500 MB.
- Drawings, CAD and lines: DWG and DXF drawings are placed as line work (lines, polylines with arcs, arcs, circles, ellipses, splines and texts of the model space; block references and dimensions expanded; paper space left out) in a neutral ink for both themes, at their own coordinates (the unit from `$INSUNITS`) or calibrated like any drawing, with a list of layers to show or hide. While dragging a point, Snap also snaps to the visible drawings' end points and crossings, else to a point on a line, with a CAD-style marker (also PDF pages' vector lines; a spatial index keeps 50 000 segments smooth). Pick lines from drawing… picks a drawing's polylines (chained end to end; Shift+click adds, a window picks all inside) and uses them as the exterior boundary (offering to close a small gap), material boundaries or water lines, clipped to the crop, each one undo step. New models from drawing… frames each section of a sheet (across a PDF's pages), names it from the text in it, calibrates it (later frames reuse the first one's scale) and makes one model per frame with the drawing placed under it.
- File › Import › .gsz file and File › Export › .gsz file: .gsz project files (a ZIP with the model as XML). The import makes a model of each stability analysis: outline, material boundaries and regions from the file's regions; Mohr-Coulomb, undrained, bedrock and high-strength materials (any other strength model reported with its parameters); piezometric lines per material; surcharges as unit weight × height above the ground; line loads, method, slices, direction and entry/exit ranges; the file's solved critical circle as a custom surface. The export writes the same back. What does not carry over is listed after each import or export. File › Import DXF is now File › Import › DXF file.
- File › Import › .stix file and File › Export › .stix file: .stix project files (a ZIP of JSON documents). The import makes a model of each calculation from the stage it calculates: layers (a layer inside another as a hole), soils (Mohr-Coulomb with the advanced model's dilatancy as Davis's reduction, Su as SHANSEP with the layer's POP, OCR or yield stress, the Su table, the sigma-tau table as a shear-normal function, another model above the phreatic level), the stress history of an earlier stage, the phreatic line and the head and reference lines, uniform loads with their degree of consolidation, line loads, the earthquake coefficients, geotextiles, the method and the file's slip plane. The export writes the same back, an earlier stage as a stage of its own. On public example files, the factor of safety on each file's own slip plane is within 0.12 % for the Bishop and Spencer calculations of a tutorial dike (five calculations, in files of five versions), a berm, a test file with every feature and one calculation of an older example; on five other calculations of two files saved before 2024 it is 0.5–2.6 % lower, and 6.9 % lower on one. An Uplift-Van slip plane, analyzed with Spencer, differs by 2–17 %. Imported, exported and imported again, every model gives the same factor of safety.
- File › Import › .sli/.slim/.slmd file and File › Export › .slim file: model files in this text format (a .slim or .slmd is a ZIP holding it; each scenario of a .slmd becomes a model). The import carries the regions (from the file's cells), Mohr-Coulomb, undrained, shear-normal, Hoek-Brown, SHANSEP and anisotropic materials, water tables and piezometric lines with Hu = 1 or automatic, rapid drawdown, distributed and line loads, seismic coefficients, bars, tiebacks and geosynthetics, the pore pressure ratio rᵤ, the method, slices, direction, given surfaces, slope limits and a tension crack. On published verification cases, the factor of safety on each reference circle is within 0.15 % for 24 of 29 results (the two with rᵤ within 0.05 %); the hand-calculated examples (7 slices) are 1.6–2.1 % lower, as the analysis uses at least 10 slices. Exported and imported again, every model gives the same factor of safety.
- B̄ (excess pore pressure from fill and loads) in every import and export: .sli files' B-bar method (B̄ on its materials, the others as fill, B̄ × second-stage loads; on the format's tutorial the four scenarios give 2.292, 1.764, 1.462 and 1.332 against 2.30, 1.77, 1.47 and 1.33), .stix files' degree of consolidation under the weight of layers (as B̄ = 1 − the degree; written back the same way), and the .gsz export (B̄ and the fill flag with the B-bar option; the import already read them).
- Seven verification problems with rᵤ (now that the engine has it): Fredlund & Krahn (1977) cases 3 and 4 (V044b, V046c), Jiang, Baker & Yamagami (2003) with a power-law envelope (V100), Li & Lumb (1987) with Hassan & Wolff's reliability index (V101), Loukidis et al. (2003) at the critical seismic coefficient, dry and rᵤ = 0.5 (V102a, V102b) and Spencer's slope (V103); all 30 new checks within tolerance (179 problems, 406 checks). Mean absolute error against the papers 1.003 → 0.942 %.
- Pore pressure ratio rᵤ on any material (Material › rᵤ): the pore pressure at a slice base is rᵤ times the weight of the soil above it per unit width (Bishop & Morgenstern 1960), added to that of its water line; also in the overburden of reinforcement. Imported from and exported to .sli files; on the format's two published verification cases with rᵤ, 1.001 against 1.001 and 0.987 against 0.988 on the reference circles. The finite element analyses do not take it.
- Waternet pore pressure: head lines and reference lines, the head varying linearly between reference lines and from the uppermost one to the phreatic level. A strength above a material's water line (the phreatic level), SHANSEP with a POP or a yield stress, an earlier stage whose effective stresses set the yield stress, and excess pore pressure under a distributed load not yet consolidated (per material, below the water).
- Material models: Mohr–Coulomb, undrained, strength increasing with depth (from the ground or from the top of the layer) or from a datum, with a strength cutoff, anisotropic, spatially varying, lime-cement columns, and high-strength and impenetrable (bedrock) layers. Saturated unit weight below the water table.
- Strength from suction above the water table (extended Mohr-Coulomb, Fredlund, Morgenstern & Widger 1978): φb and an optional maximum suction on Mohr-Coulomb, the anisotropic function and the drained branch of a combined S = f(depth). Above the material's piezometric line the pore pressure is −γw × the head (as below it, with the phreatic correction when on), and (−u)·tan φb adds to c′ on the slice; with partial factors it takes γc′. The finite element analyses take it at their Gauss points. The slice table and plots show the negative pore pressure. The .gsz import reads UnsatPhiB as φb and the piezometric line's CapSuction/MaxSuction as the maximum suction of the materials on it, and the export writes them; a suction strength from a function of the volumetric water content is reported as not imported. On the format's tutorial example (φb = 10°) the factor of safety on the file's critical circle is 1.371 against the file's 1.372 (1.319 without φb).
- Anisotropy on the undrained strengths (undrained, from depth, from datum): Su times a factor of the slice base inclination, interpolated from points [α, factor] (for example passive 0.82 at −45°, direct 1.0 at 0°, active 1.5 at 60°), on top of the strength's variation with depth and its cutoff. The anisotropic model took a constant strength only, so that a clay whose Su varies with depth needed thin layers of it. Finite elements and limit analysis take the factor at α = 0.
- Combined S = f(depth): strength increasing with depth limited by a drained c′ + σ′·tan φ′, c′ increasing with the same depth (from the ground or the top of the layer) at its own rate, as clay in a Swedish combined analysis. Partial factors take γcu on Su and γc′, γφ′ on the drained branch; anisotropy applies to Su; the SGI 3D end faces take Su, as they now also do for combined Mohr-Coulomb clay.
- Water: several piezometric lines with one per material if needed, ponded water above the ground.
- Ponded water above the ground comes from the line of the material at the ground, the line that also gives that material its pore pressure, in the analysis (weight on the ground, horizontal thrust, tension-crack depth) and in the drawings; a material's own line above the ground used to give the base the pore pressure of the pond without its weight.
- Distributed and line loads; anchors, grouted tiebacks, soil nails, geosynthetics, pile rows and sheet pile walls.
- A warning, with any method, when the critical surface enters steeper than 70° and slices on that steep part hold only by tension at the base (N′ < 0 or a low m_α), pointing to the crack at a base angle: Bishop and Janbu still give a factor there, on a φ = 0 slope with a vertical upper face Bishop 0.460 against 0.426 with the steep part cut off at 70°.
- A warning when Spencer or Morgenstern-Price finds no complete equilibrium (no solution, or only the closest pair on the critical surface) on a surface entering steeper than 70°, pointing to the crack at a base angle: cohesion along a nearly vertical base can only hold the slice with tension. On a φ = 0 slope with a vertical upper face, Spencer finds no solution on a circle entering at 82° and, with a 70° crack angle, the same factor as Bishop.
- Tension crack at a limiting base inclination (Options › Crack at base angle): from the entry, the part of a slip surface steeper than the angle is cut off and the surface ends at a vertical crack there; with a crack depth too, the crack further from the entry governs. .gsz tension cracks with the angle option are imported and exported.
- Composite circular slip surfaces (Options › Circles follow bedrock, on by default; …and the base of the model, off by default and on for imported .gsz files): a circle that reaches impenetrable material on the base of the model, or optionally the bare base itself, follows its top there instead of being rejected; the slices along it have straight bases analyzed about the circle's center. With the base followed, on the James Bay dyke (V039) the search gives 1.166 against Duncan & Wright's composite surface 1.17 (circles alone 1.45); on a public .gsz example whose critical circle runs 18 cm below the model base, 1.2792 on that circle against the file's 1.2799 (the circle was rejected before; the search gave 1.324, now 1.277). Verification checks against circle-only references analyze circles only (V038–V040, V073). Following the base is not the default: in models whose base is only the edge of the model it lowers the factor of safety along a boundary that is not in the ground (in the verification set by up to 2.4 %).
- SHANSEP material: Su = σ′v·S·OCR^m with OCR constant or against elevation, or Su against σ′v, σ′v the slice's weight and loads per width less its pore pressure. B̄ pore pressure: a material's pore pressure rises by B̄ times the weight of the fills above it. Shear-normal function material: τ against σ′n as points, the tangent c and φ at each base's normal stress, updated by the analysis. Tables of these models can be cubic splines (natural, or with given end slopes). Imported from .gsz files with their splines, B-bar pore pressure and S = f(datum) for a cohesion that is a straight-line function of elevation: on the format's SHANSEP example all five analyses within 0.17 % on the files' surfaces, the shear-normal example within 0.01 %.
- Pseudostatic seismic load (Options › Seismic, kh and kv): kh·W at each slice's center of gravity in the sliding direction, kv·W vertically. Imported from .gsz files: three levee models with kh = 0.12 within 0.3–0.7 % (before, the factors were up to 30 % too high without the seismic load).
- Inclined line loads (angle from the vertical, and the elevation of the load point on a wall face): the horizontal part acts on the slice under the load. Imported from .gsz files: the format's active and passive earth pressure examples within 0.07 %.
- Rapid drawdown, three-stage (Duncan, Wright & Wong 1990): the water before drawdown and a drawdown (R) envelope on the materials that do not drain; the undrained strength at the consolidation stresses before drawdown, interpolated by Kc between the R and effective envelopes, or the drained strength where lower. On the format's Walter Bouldin Dam example 1.041 against the file's 1.026 (+1.5 %; the file interpolates its undrained strengths slightly lower). Imported from .gsz files.
- .gsz import of reinforcement: anchors (bond over π × the bond diameter along the bonded length, the head on the wall), nails (bond along the whole length, the plate capacity at the head), geosynthetics (the pullout resistance as given, or from the overburden with the interface angle and surface area factor) and piles (the shear force along the slip surface). "F of S dependent" elements are passive, the force orientation (along the element, the slip surface or between) is kept, and older files' reduction factors are applied. A distributed force is applied where the slip surface crosses the element; a user-defined force (a function of distance) is not imported. On the format's four reinforcement examples, on the files' own surfaces: anchors within 0.2 % (four analyses), nails within 0.15 % (three), geosynthetics within 0.4 % (three); the piles add 0.134 to the factor of safety against the file's 0.137. When a .gsz file reports the moment axis as the center of a circle (an entry and exit search with an axis), the circle's center is taken from the ends of its slices.
- Reinforcement: the bond along an element develops only where the element is in the soil (a nail head given beyond the face develops nothing in the air), as the interface friction already did; an element within 10° of the normal to the slip surface carries its force (the movement neither pulls nor pushes it appreciably), as commercial programs apply it whatever the angle.
- Hoek-Brown with the criterion's own parameters mb, s and a given directly (for example the original criterion, a = 0.5) instead of GSI, mi and D; .gsz files with Hoek-Brown in either form are imported (on the format's example 1.1773 and 1.7094 on the file's circles against 1.1780 and 1.7102; its open-pit example within 0.1 %).
- An exit range starting at a corner of the ground (the toe), or being the toe alone, is moved upslope by 1e-5 of the model width: it now holds the circles exiting just above the toe (on the format's Hoek-Brown example the search found 1.238 with its exit range starting at the toe, now 1.1625; the file 1.178).
- A search range given as a single point (an exit at the toe, as .gsz files give it) is searched just beside the point: a circle exactly through a corner of the ground only touches it and runs on in the soil, so it never exited there. On the format's tension crack example (exit at the toe) the search gives 1.2521 against the file's 1.2535 (1.331 before), and 1.2242, 1.2214 and 1.1799 against 1.2250, 1.2209 and 1.1805 with a crack angle, a crack line and a water-filled crack.
- .gsz import and export: the tension crack angle is counter-clockwise from +x as the format has it (120° for sliding to the right is a base inclination of 60°), the water in a crack is the fraction filled (1 = full), and the default Entry and Exit search (written without its option) and an entry or exit given as a point are read as search limits. Files of the format's version 8 (2012: GeometryItems, SlopeItems, piezometric lines on the geometry's points) are read, and an analysis that cannot be read (no geometry or materials) is named in the notes instead of stopping the import of the others. Fully specified slip surfaces are imported as custom surfaces with their moment axis and analyzed alone (on the format's example 1.1306 against the file's 1.1298). A block search is imported as cSlope's block search: each grid becomes a window and its end angles the inclinations of the end segments, with the optimization (1.5651 and 1.2820 against 1.5694 and 1.2756 on the format's example). S = f(depth) from the top of the layer (SFnDepth) and an anisotropy function on an undrained strength are imported. Block end angles outside 0–90° (an exit segment dipping down) leave the Rankine angle with a note, one given angle is a fixed angle, and the results of an analysis with a "/" in its name are found.
- Slices of a circle follow the circle: the base length is the arc length, the inclination that at the middle of the arc and the weight that of the soil above the arc (the base was the tangent at the slice's x-midpoint with length b/cos α, which under-credited cohesion on steep slices, and the weight under one arc node). The steep ends of a circle are cut into narrower slices, none spanning more than twice the arc of a slice at the bottom of the circle or 2°, whichever is more. Against 4000 slices on the verification's 150 fixed-circle checks the largest error at the default slice count drops from 0.87 % (V068, a vertical tangent at the crest) to 0.16 %, with 3 % more slices; on the .gsz format's examples the factor on the file's circle comes closer to the file's (Mohr–Coulomb examples 1.349, 1.267 and 1.135 against 1.351, 1.268 and 1.137).
- Distributed loads normal to the ground (Direction in the UDL table): water or earth pressure against a slope, its vertical part q per horizontal meter and its horizontal part q·tan β into the ground, on the slices (force and moment), in the finite element strength reduction (normal traction) and in the SGI 3D driving moment; the limit analysis bounds take the vertical part. .gsz surcharges acting normal to the ground are imported and exported as such.
- Edit view: coordinate labels find a free place outside the fill, clear of other labels, the boundary lines, entry/exit dots, markers, loads, elements, water lines, water symbols and custom surfaces, and points off the screen get none; vertex dots are drawn over the search-limit markers; interior boundaries are drawn only inside the model; a custom circle shows dashed only from where it enters the ground to where it leaves it, on a halo that keeps it visible on dark layers; a fixed tension-crack depth shows as a labeled dimension on the crest instead of a dashed line along the whole ground; with only custom surfaces analyzed the search limits are hidden; long ruler labels are set smaller, and left out only when they still do not fit, so no label is cut; load handles are picked over the arrow height as drawn.
- Engine: `Slope.toModelX` and `Slope.toModelCircle` take the analyzed surfaces (`search`, in the canonical left-to-right space of the analysis) back to the model space in which the definition and its custom surfaces are given; for a right-to-left slope a searched circle passed back as a custom surface without them landed mirrored on the other side of the model. The coordinate spaces are documented on `search` and `customSearchPlanes` (`analyse` with `mapAnalysisResultToModelSpace` gives whole results in model space).

### Results and reporting

- The paper takes in the critical circle's center and FOS label when they are shown, widening a fitted frame after a run if needed.
- Critical surface, all surfaces or a factor-of-safety filter, slices, annotations and templates.
- Plots of slice data and of Fm and Ff against λ; export to PDF and PNG, slice data to Excel and CSV.
- Result warnings, in the results panel and the PDF summary: a material boundary that ends inside a region (it does not split it), and slices of the critical surface with negative effective normal force (N′ < 0) or m_α below 0.2. Boundary ends within 1e-3 of the model size of the outline or another boundary are put on it, so a layer is no longer lost to a small gap.
- A result warning when the critical surface lies on the edge of the search limits: its entry or exit at the end of its range, or with the block search an end angle at the end of its range or a base point at the end of a search line. Ends on the model's edge, on a corner of the ground or at a load's edge, and ranges of one point, are not reported. On the verification problems it is given for V047b only (entry at the end of the source's crest range; widening the range lowers F from 1.659 to 1.658).
- The PDF report ends with an analysis summary: method, search, water and tension crack, partial factors, materials and loads. With partial factors, the PDF and the slice data mark design values. The Export button's menu turns the summary pages off (they are included by default) and has a title line with the model name, factor of safety and method above the drawing (on by default, so that page 1 printed on its own carries the result; it can be turned off); both choices are saved with the model's result view.
- The paper frames the model at true scale with a margin, including water lines, loads and reinforcement, and spaces its axis ticks for their labels; PNG export is 2400 px wide on white. "Use model bounds" and the refit after the geometry grows give the same frame the model starts with, below the title block. Fit in the edit view ignores water lines far below the model, and the parts of a water line below its base, and keeps clear of the toolbar.
- Result plot: the sliding mass follows stepped and vertical faces, a tension crack is drawn as part of the critical surface with the water in it, slice lines show on dark layers, and the search limits point into their ranges under the result.
- Loads and reinforcement forces on the canvas and in the PDF: touching load strips draw as one load with one label ("q = 20–40 kPa"), box heights follow the magnitude and magnitudes are rounded; force arrows sit beside the element they act along, on one side and within 30 % of the spacing in a dense wall so each reads as its own element's, clear of the slip line, the element itself, material boundaries, other elements and each other and never longer than the element; each force value is written at its element's head, outside the slope on the element's line, or beside its arrow when the slip surface crosses the element far from the head, and where elements are closer than the text height the values form a column with a leader to each element number, so no value is left out ("÷ FOS", drawn so it cannot read as "+", marks a force divided by the factor of safety; forces of 0.0 kN/m are left out); force labels keep clear of the search-limit markers and a margin inside the frame; element numbers, on a light halo, keep clear of a vertex dot at the head and move off lines and markers.
- Water-table symbols, one or two per line on its flat parts, prefer open water, then the soil outside the sliding mass, keep clear of the critical surface, vertex dots, prescribed surfaces, search markers, loads, reinforcement, thin layers and the model's sides, and leave out the ≡ where the water is too shallow for it. Over a result the search-limit triangles stand above the ground, a limit shared by both ranges is drawn once and no dots lie on the sliding mass. A tension crack is drawn only where the analysis cut the surface at one, with its water in the crack; a thin sliding mass is widened only under the ground, coincident or dense slice lines are thinned, and outlines have no mitre spikes. The PDF draws loads, search markers and water symbols as the canvas does.
- Ponded water shows small load arrows pointing at the ground, normal to it and scaled with the depth, clear of the water-table symbols, on the canvas and in the PDF.
- Finite element results of a model facing left (crest on the right) lie where the model is: the plastic zone was drawn mirrored, out over the lower ground, because the app gave the finite element analysis the definition already mirrored to slide to the right, which it mirrored once more. It now gets the model as drawn and runs on it mirrored, as the engine's other callers; the factor of safety of such a model can differ slightly from before (another mesh).
- Finite element results: an "FE mesh" button in the Results ribbon draws the final mesh, thin under the plastic zone, to show where it was refined (off by default; kept in the project, as is a hidden plastic zone, which a saved project showed again before).
- Custom search planes in the result: the results panel and the PDF summary list each custom surface with its factor of safety, or why it has none (the circle misses the ground, the surface leaves the model, is too shallow, passes through impenetrable material, or did not converge); the engine marks each surface analyzed for a custom plane with it. A "Custom surfaces" toggle in the Surfaces menu draws them dashed, each with its own factor label, on the canvas and in the PDF (off by default; kept with the result view). They are numbered "Custom surface 1", "2", … as the analysis takes them: the circles first, then the polylines.
- Results › Properties: a Grid checkbox with a grid spacing of its own (empty: the tick spacing) replaces the Grid button in the ribbon.
- Plot annotations have round ticks (1, 2 or 5 × 10ⁿ apart) with values on both axes and grid lines at the ticks, on the canvas and in the PDF; they showed only each axis's minimum and maximum.
- Over a result the search-limit markers look as in the edit view, on the canvas and in the PDF. The slip surface, radius lines, model outline, slices, material boundaries, loads, water lines and material table are drawn with thinner lines.
- The Material table and Color bar buttons toggle (a second click added a second table or bar); the color bar starts at the right edge of the sheet instead of the middle, right below the material table as it is drawn (its height for the materials, columns, text size and paper; to its left when there is no room below), so the two no longer overlap with many materials. The color bar has a range (automatic, fixed, or the surface filter's) and a color scheme of its own, which also color the surfaces on the sheet.
- Reference lines (Results › Annotations › Reference line): polylines and circles in model coordinates drawn over the result and in the exports, for example a slip surface from another program; a custom search plane can be copied as one. They are saved with the model and left out of view templates.
- Drawing details: in the edit view a search-limit triangle at a vertex moves off the vertex dot; with a fixed tension crack a custom surface starts at the crack's base, as the analysis takes it, and the crack dimension stands at that crack (otherwise on the crest with its bottom clear of layer boundaries), labeled next to it; slice boundaries closer than 2.5 px (0.7 mm in the PDF) are drawn as one line; the band of a thin sliding mass ends at the slip line's ends.

### Interface

- Edit canvas: select an object, then drag its handles, whatever window is open (an open window no longer chooses what can be edited). Every object can be selected: the exterior, material boundaries, water lines, loads, reinforcement and piles, the search limits and custom surfaces. Hovering highlights the object under the pointer; a click selects it, shows its handles and names it in the status bar; a drag over an object that is not selected moves nothing, and a drag starts only after 4 px. Loads and custom surfaces also move whole by their body. A click on a point of the selected object selects the point, repeated clicks at one spot cycle through the objects there, Escape or a click on empty canvas clears the selection; the arrow keys nudge the selected point by one snap step (Shift: ten), Delete removes it, and a double-click opens an editor for its exact coordinates. Right-click an object to lock or unlock it, or empty canvas for Lock all by type and Unlock all: a locked object can be selected but not dragged, nudged or have points deleted. Imported models start with their geometry locked, with Unlock in the status bar. Locks are saved with each model. The windows of the Model tab show the canvas selection as their selected row, and a row picked in a window selects it on the canvas. Reference lines in the result view are selected before they are dragged too.
- Sensitivity and Probabilistic open in windows of their own, like Results plot, instead of panels over the ribbon: they can stay open beside the model and follow its edits, and their analyses still run in the main window.
- One place for the paper's settings: Results › Display › Sheet settings selects the paper, opens the result sidebar and brings the paper's properties into view (size, orientation, extents, ticks and grid), instead of opening a View settings window with the same fields.
- Tooltips say what a field is, its unit and its default in at most about 200 characters; the explanations and references they carried (finite element flow rule, solver, convergence, initial stresses, limit analysis bounds, rapid drawdown, anisotropy, pile stiffness and more) are in the documentation, which a "?" at each section of Options and of the other dialog windows opens at the right heading.
- A failed run shows its error over the edit canvas and in the status bar, with a shortcut to the search limits when no slip surface was found. F5 or Ctrl+Enter runs the analysis; Escape closes the dialog windows and Model properties. A new project starts with a neutral "Model 1" instead of an example's name and title.
- One analysis runs on several cores (one worker per core, up to 8), with the same result as on one. Run all analyzes several models at a time, the longest expected first, on workers kept for the next run.
- A run with finite elements shows the limit equilibrium result as soon as it is ready while the finite elements go on, and the status bar names their stage with the time since the run started: "Finite elements: strength reduction, trial F = 1.23 (5)", "upper bound, mesh 2 of up to 4", "Davis B lower bound" (the engine reports each stage as it starts; the results are the same). Cancel stops the run at any stage.
- Number fields read a decimal comma in any app language ("0,5" in an English locale was read as 5).
- Options: the finite element analysis is turned on with "Run strength reduction (FE)"; its settings show only once it is on.
- Models list: a dot before each model shows its run state (green analyzed, blue running, red failed, hollow gray not analyzed, an amber ring when the result is out of date); the active model is shown by the row background. An out-of-date result also says so at the top of the results summary, on page 1 and the summary pages of the PDF, and in the first row of the slice data export.
- Side panels collapse from a button in their own header row to a strip at the window edge that opens them again; the activity bar is gone.
- Ribbon: in-house icons, with technical symbols for loads, water and annotations.
- Windows: the main window keeps a native frame under its hidden title bar, so the content no longer jumps when a dialog window opens or closes.
- Projects are saved as .cslp, a ZIP archive of JSON files (the project, one folder per model with its result, attachments and a thumbnail; docs/file-format.md), written the same way every time so saves can be compared; .json and .cslope projects from earlier versions open as before. The installer registers .cslp, so double-clicking a project opens it in cSlope (in the running cSlope, if one is open).
- File › Save as anonymized… saves a copy of the project to share, for example in a public issue: without the project information, drawings and their files, with generic names for models, materials, water lines and other objects, the free text of annotations replaced (variables such as #FOS kept), and optionally without results or moved to a local origin (every coordinate and datum elevation shifted alike, so the factor of safety is the same). The copy has no thumbnail or original file names; the open project and its file are not changed.
- Security: imported ZIP archives are checked against size limits before they are unpacked, and files above 50 MB are refused; .gsz files with a DOCTYPE are refused; project files keep only the fields the program knows; a stricter content security policy; the packaged app refuses to start when its archive differs from the one it was built with (asar integrity), and cannot be used to run other scripts.

### In the browser

- cSlope in the browser at app.cslope.com, linked from the website's home and download pages: two templates. The excavation in clay with a haul road has ground level, depth and slope, a one-sided excavation or a trench of a given bottom width, horizontal layers with the app's material models (Undrained, with Su constant or increasing with depth; Mohr-Coulomb; Impenetrable (bedrock)), a ground-water level and an optional haul road with a traffic load; the embankment on clay has its height, crest width, slopes, layers, water and a load on the crest. Both are analyzed by the same engine on the user's own computer as soon as the form is complete. It shows the section with the critical surface and F with its method; the address holds the parameters, so a link opens the same section, and the analysis downloads as JSON.
- In the browser, F is always a factor of safety on characteristic values: the Safety section (characteristic or design values, safety class, η and γQ/γd) is removed from both templates. Design values with partial factors are for the app and the Python package.
- In the browser, each layer is entered by its thickness instead of its bottom level: the layers stack down from the ground level, and the header of each shows its thickness with its levels ("13.5 m (−1.5 to −15 m)"). The headers have buttons to move a layer up or down and to insert one above it; the layers below follow. Each layer has a color, chosen from 14 swatches (the app's six material colors first), drawn in the section and written into the analysis document.
- In the browser, the result card no longer lists the engine's warnings (the app shows them): the excavation uses the engine's automatic tension crack (its depth from the soil at each entry, and at a base steeper than 65°), so that a steep entry relying on tension at the base is cut off, and the haul road's sides are 1:2.5 instead of 1:1.5, so that a shallow slide down a cohesionless fill does not govern. F of the excavation's examples moves by −6.0 % to +0.3 % (clay over rock 1.530 → 1.438); the embankment's is unchanged (no crack: in its cohesionless fill a crack raised F).
- In the browser, the excavation opens with typical values (a dry crust over a soft clay over sand and gravel, and a haul road with traffic), so that the page computes F at once; the embankment's strengths still start empty.
- In the browser: while the layers cannot be built (a thickness empty or wrong, a removed layer leaving the rest invalid), the drawing shows only the section's outline, greyed, with "Fix the layers to draw the section." instead of the last layers; "Open in cSlope" is "Get the cSlope app"; the phone's result bar says "Minimum factor of safety" as the card does; an undrained Su must be more than 0; after a layer is moved to the top or the bottom the focus goes to its other move button; the γ fields say to enter the saturated unit weight below the water level.
- In the browser: a removed layer or a reset to the defaults can be undone from the message that reports it (on a phone, a notice above the result bar); the drawing shows the model's whole depth with its firm base dashed; on a phone the result bar has a thumbnail of the section that opens the drawing full screen.
- In the browser, **Download .cslp** saves the section as a cSlope project, which the app opens with the model (its layers in their colors, the template's options) and the page's result, current; the app's own run gives the same F. A result that is not yet the section's (the analysis running, a change before its run) is left out, and the page says so. The engine's project writer is a WebAssembly module of its own (about 2.1 MB, 0.4 MB compressed), loaded only when a project is downloaded, so the engine stays within its 4 MB budget.

### Python

- The Python package `cslope` (`pip install cslope`, CPython 3.10 and later on Windows, macOS and Linux) builds a model or opens a .cslp project, runs it with the same engine as the app and saves a project the app opens with the result current; a `cslope` command validates and runs analysis documents.

### Verification

- 187 verification problems from the literature and from other programs' verification manuals, run by the automated tests on every change.
- Inputs corrected against the program manuals' figures: the water table of the Sharma (1996) slope (V082a–c, 1.1–1.7 ft lower), the lacustrine clay of the James Bay dyke 6.5 m thick (V039, V040), phreatic correction on ACADS 4 (V051) and the circle radius of the USACE dam (V087, 169.0 ft). Program values computed on the earlier inputs are no longer checks. A further input audit of all 164 problems against their sources corrected V005, V009a–c, V017a/b, V042, V061, V065, V068, V075, V077, V083 and V092, and the reference or check of V029, V060a, V066c and V098. More published values are checks: Morgenstern–Price (half-sine) on V082b, V082c, V086b, V086c, V088–V090, V092, V096a and V096b, Janbu simplified on V085 and V093, and a new problem V082d with the program's circular search between exit limits; V095b, a duplicate of V095a, is merged into it; V016c's block-search values, which belong to a search type cSlope does not have, are no longer checks.
- A published value that is confirmed in its source but contradicts the source's own rigorous results for the same surface is shown as disputed, with the reason, and left out of the error statistics (Zhu & Lee's simplified Bishop value 1.429 for surface 3).
